What turns payment activity into evidence that a business can repay?

Moniepoint’s cash-flow lending proposition connects with the question behind Underwrite for Collection: how do we design credit around sustainable repayment throughout a facility’s life?

My latest article follows a synthetic merchant from transaction records to a reviewed facility, receipts and lender contribution.

Three questions guide the work:

1. Which account credits are recurring business cash, and which are transfers, owner support, borrowing or reversals?
2. How should uncertainty and the cash calendar shape the amount, payment dates and human review?
3. What happens after disbursement when receipts arrive late, trading weakens or a data feed fails?

The example starts with KES500,000 in credits. After classification, KES410,000 represents customer receipts. Operating, household and existing debt commitments leave KES25,000 before the proposed loan.

A KES35,000 monthly payment prompts a revised proposal. The case tests a KES30,000 facility with three KES11,000 instalments, using explicit uncertainty, cash-reserve and review assumptions.

Ten reproducible scenarios follow source errors, settlement delays, repeat applications, schedule changes and workout. Thirty-six tests reconcile the records. Named charts show cash, uncertainty, dues, receipts and lender economics.

An approved extension can remove contractual arrears while the merchant still has a household cash shortfall. Both belong in the outcome record.

The detailed agentic workflow connects evidence intake, replayable calculations, versioned policy rules, maker–checker decisions and monitoring. It draws on the whitepaper and the five-article research series.

I welcome discussion with Moniepoint and lenders interested in testing this approach with approved data and operating rules.
