# Detailed Publication Plan for *Underwrite for Collection*

## 1. Purpose and intended outcome

This plan establishes a controlled publication pathway for *Underwrite for Collection: Rebuilding Credit Architecture Around Sustainable Cash Recovery*. Its purpose is to give the paper the strongest possible opportunity for formal recognition while preserving the option to reach banking, fintech, credit-risk, collections, accounting, and Kenyan financial-sector audiences through practitioner channels.

The primary objective is to prepare and submit a publication-specific version to the *Journal of Risk Management in Financial Institutions*. The current practitioner article will remain the canonical source from which the journal manuscript is developed. It will not be overwritten. The journal submission will be maintained as a separate manuscript with its own abstract, structure, references, declarations, cover letter, and version history.

The preferred sequence is:

1. Prepare the journal manuscript without replacing the existing practitioner article.
2. Publish only a short LinkedIn post containing the central question, selected insights, and a concise synopsis while formal publication is being considered.
3. Submit the expanded manuscript exclusively to the *Journal of Risk Management in Financial Institutions*.
4. Disclose any related online material to the editor.
5. If accepted, share only the version or link permitted by the publication agreement.
6. If the journal route is declined or no longer preferred, publish the practitioner edition through a LinkedIn Newsletter and adapt it for the Kenya Institute of Bankers or Finextra.
7. Consider the Kenya Bankers Association and SSRN routes after the empirical and methodological content has been strengthened appropriately.

This sequence preserves the chance of peer-reviewed publication without sacrificing later industry reach.

## 2. Primary publication target

### 2.1 Selected journal

The strongest formal home is the *Journal of Risk Management in Financial Institutions*. It publishes peer-reviewed best-practice articles, applied research, case studies, new approaches, regulatory updates, and other work relevant to risk professionals in financial institutions. Its readership includes professionals working in banking risk, financial regulation, credit, investment management, market infrastructure, and related disciplines.

The paper fits this remit because it connects credit approval to post-disbursement monitoring, sustainable collection, responsible artificial intelligence, expected credit loss, economic capital, recovery, and structured-finance cash flows. Its central proposition is operational and relevant to risk management practice:

> A loan is successfully underwritten only when its structure, monitoring and intervention design support sustainable collection after disbursement.

The journal accepts articles and papers of approximately 2,500 to 6,000 words. The current article falls within that range. The journal is indexed in Scopus and RePEc, giving the work greater formal discoverability than a stand-alone social-media article. All submissions are subject to anonymous peer review.

Official author guidance: <https://henrystewartpublications.com/journal/journal-of-risk-management-in-financial-institutions/instructions-for-authors/>

### 2.2 Publication-specific manuscript

The journal version will be created as a separate Word manuscript. It will not silently replace the existing Markdown, Word, or PDF practitioner editions. The working title may remain *Underwrite for Collection*, although a more descriptive subtitle can be used to foreground the manuscript's contribution to financial-institution risk management.

The manuscript will contain:

- A title page with author name, contact details, and any appropriate affiliation.
- An abstract of no more than 300 words.
- Up to seven keywords.
- A predominantly third-person professional style.
- A clearly defined problem statement.
- A concise statement of the original or distinctive contribution.
- A practical case study, worked example, empirical illustration, or controlled numerical demonstration.
- Clear implications for banking and credit-risk practice.
- References formatted in the journal's Vancouver system.
- A short author biography of no more than 75 words.
- A conflict-of-interest statement where appropriate.
- A dedicated declaration describing the use of artificial intelligence in preparing the manuscript.
- Any tables and figures in editable or publication-quality form.

The final submission will be sent in Microsoft Word format to the publisher, Julie Kerry, at `julie@hspublications.co.uk`.

## 3. Manuscript development requirements

### 3.1 Abstract and keywords

The abstract will summarise the problem addressed, the approach taken, the principal findings or propositions, and the implications for financial institutions. It will not operate as a general introduction. It must state what the paper contributes and how a lender could apply the proposed architecture.

The final keyword set will contain no more than seven terms. Suitable candidates include:

- Credit risk.
- Collections.
- Behavioural monitoring.
- Responsible artificial intelligence.
- Expected credit loss.
- Recovery.
- Structured finance.

The terms will be selected to represent the final emphasis of the journal manuscript rather than merely repeating words from the title.

### 3.2 Style conversion

The existing article is deliberately written in a direct, narrative practitioner voice. The journal manuscript will preserve its clarity and human context while converting most first-person and conversational statements into a professional third-person presentation.

This conversion will not flatten the central story. The opening contrast between celebrated disbursement and the less visible work of collection will remain. The Kenyan banking evidence will continue to anchor the discussion. The revised style will simply make the argument suitable for anonymous peer review and a formal risk-management journal.

Acronyms will be written out on first appearance. Terminology such as non-performing loan, expected credit loss, special-purpose vehicle, probability of default, loss given default, and exposure at default will be introduced consistently before abbreviations are used.

### 3.3 Practical evidence and worked example

The main development requirement is a stronger practical demonstration. The current article presents a comprehensive lifecycle architecture, but a formal journal submission will be more persuasive if it shows how the architecture changes an actual decision or portfolio outcome.

The worked example may use anonymised, public, or clearly labelled synthetic data. It should compare a conventional origination-centred process with a collection-aware process. The example should demonstrate some or all of the following:

- How pre-disbursement affordability assumptions are aligned with observed cash-flow cadence.
- How behavioural indicators change after disbursement.
- How repayment velocity, earnings velocity, wallet volatility, cash-flow asymmetry, and debt-to-liquidity measures identify emerging stress.
- How a conventional scorecard and an artificial-intelligence representation are compared rather than assumed to have equal value.
- How policy actions differ across stable, deteriorating, curable, and recovery-bound accounts.
- How cure, modification, prepayment, delinquency, default, and recovery states are represented.
- How collection timing and recovery costs affect expected credit loss.
- How the same realised cash outcomes affect reserves, economic capital, funding, and a ring-fenced special-purpose vehicle waterfall.

Potential output measures include:

- First-payment default.
- Roll rates and transition probabilities.
- Cash collection efficiency.
- Time to deterioration.
- Cure rate and durable cure rate.
- Net recovery value after direct workout cost.
- Expected loss and lifetime expected credit loss.
- Calibration by origination vintage.
- Intervention uplift relative to an untreated comparison.
- Change in economic-capital consumption.
- Effect on reserve sufficiency and debt-service coverage.

The numerical demonstration must distinguish illustrative results from empirical findings. If synthetic data are used, the paper will state that the experiment demonstrates mechanics and computational feasibility rather than making factual claims about Kenyan borrowers.

### 3.4 References

The present IEEE-style numbered references will be converted to the journal's Vancouver format. Citations will continue to appear numerically in order of first use, but the journal uses superscripted references and has detailed rules for repeated sources.

The conversion process will verify:

- Every factual or regulatory claim has an appropriate source.
- Every in-text citation resolves to one reference-list entry.
- References follow first appearance.
- Web sources include the institution or author, title, year where available, full URL, and access date.
- Repeated citations follow the journal's stated convention rather than automatically reusing the original number.
- Embedded hyperlinks are removed from body prose where the journal requires full source details in the reference list.
- No source is retained merely because it appeared in an earlier draft.

Primary and authoritative sources will remain preferred, including the Central Bank of Kenya, Kenyan legislation, Basel Committee standards, IFRS material, the World Bank, and official governance frameworks.

### 3.5 Artificial-intelligence disclosure

The journal permits the use of generative artificial intelligence and AI-assisted tools provided that authors disclose how and where they were used, review the output, accept responsibility for the paper, and ensure that no copyright or ownership rights have been infringed.

The journal manuscript will therefore include a separate section immediately before the references entitled `Use of artificial intelligence`. It will identify the tools used, describe their functions, and explain the extent of their use. It will also include the responsibility statement required by the journal:

> I have reviewed and edited the content produced by the artificial intelligence tools and am fully responsible for the information contained in the article.

The disclosure will be accurate and specific. It will distinguish assistance with research organisation, drafting, editing, citation checking, document conversion, and visual preparation from the author's intellectual contribution and final verification.

## 4. Prior publication, duplicate publication, and self-plagiarism

### 4.1 Distinct concepts

Three issues must remain separate:

- **Self-plagiarism** arises when an author substantially reuses previously published text without appropriate disclosure or acknowledgement.
- **Prior publication** concerns whether an editor regards a publicly accessible earlier version as already published and therefore ineligible for the journal.
- **Copyright and licensing** concern the rights retained, granted, or transferred when a version is published on a platform or accepted by a publisher.

Sharing an idea on LinkedIn does not automatically prevent formal publication. Publishing the complete, identical manuscript can materially restrict the available routes, especially where a journal requires original and unpublished submissions.

### 4.2 Journal restriction

The *Journal of Risk Management in Financial Institutions* states that material published elsewhere is generally not accepted unless expressly invited or agreed by the publisher. It also requires that a manuscript under review not be submitted simultaneously to another publication. Published contributions ordinarily become the publisher's copyright unless another arrangement is agreed.

For that reason, the preferred strategy is to avoid publishing the full manuscript on LinkedIn while the journal route remains active. A LinkedIn post may present:

- The core architectural question.
- The central proposition.
- A short description of the Kenyan credit-quality context.
- Several principal insights.
- An invitation for practitioner feedback.

It should not reproduce the complete article section by section.

### 4.3 If a full LinkedIn version has already been published

An earlier LinkedIn publication does not make all later use illegitimate. It changes the required process. The journal version would need to become a substantially extended manuscript rather than an undisclosed duplicate.

The revised manuscript should:

- Add empirical analysis, a controlled simulation, or a substantive numerical worked example.
- Develop the methodology and validation framework.
- Add findings, limitations, and implementation implications.
- Use a distinct title or subtitle where useful.
- State a clearer research or practice contribution.
- Cite or acknowledge the earlier practitioner-oriented version.
- Explain the relationship between the two versions in the cover letter.
- Obtain written approval from the editor before formal submission.

The editor should receive the LinkedIn URL and enough information to decide whether the revised manuscript satisfies the journal's originality policy. No assumption should be made on the author's behalf.

A suitable disclosure is:

> An earlier practitioner-oriented version of this argument appeared as a LinkedIn article. The submitted manuscript substantially extends that discussion through additional methodology, evidence, validation, and implications for financial-institution risk management. The earlier version is disclosed here for editorial consideration.

The publisher may accept, reject, or request further differentiation. Written editorial guidance will govern the next step.

## 5. Submission package

The journal submission package will contain:

1. The Microsoft Word manuscript.
2. A concise cover letter.
3. The abstract and keyword list.
4. Author name, address, contact information, and short biography.
5. Conflict-of-interest and AI-use disclosures.
6. Editable tables, charts, and diagrams where requested.
7. Details of any related online practitioner article, preprint, presentation, or working paper.
8. Confirmation that the manuscript is not simultaneously under review elsewhere.

The cover letter will explain:

- Why the paper fits the journal's professional audience.
- Its contribution to lifecycle credit-risk management.
- The practical importance of connecting origination with sustainable collection.
- The nature of the Kenyan evidence and its broader relevance.
- The role of the worked example.
- Any earlier public version and how the submission materially extends it.
- The author's responsibility for the accuracy of all facts, calculations, references, and conclusions.

## 6. Alternative publication routes

### 6.1 Kenya Institute of Bankers

The first alternative is the Kenya Institute of Bankers through *KIB Monthly* or its Bankers Journal. This route offers the strongest direct Kenyan practitioner audience. The paper's treatment of credit quality, collections, International Financial Reporting Standard 9, behavioural scoring, MSME finance, responsible AI, restructuring, and recovery is directly relevant.

The initial action will be an editorial pitch to `thebanker@kib.co.ke`, not an unsolicited assumption that the complete manuscript will be accepted. The pitch will include the title, a concise synopsis, the intended audience, the article's practical contribution, approximate word count, and a short author biography. The version supplied to KIB will follow any exclusivity, length, citation, and formatting rules provided by its editor.

### 6.2 Kenya Bankers Association Centre for Research

The Kenya Bankers Association Centre for Research on Financial Markets and Policy offers the strongest route to institutional Kenyan credibility. The appropriate contact is `research@kba.co.ke`.

The current paper would require a more research-oriented form for this route. A working-paper version should add a clear research question, literature context, data or simulation design, methodology, results, limitations, and policy implications. A numerical or empirical addition is especially important.

No current open call was identified when this plan was prepared. The first step should therefore be an editorial enquiry asking whether the Centre would consider the paper for a working-paper series, research conference, policy brief, or other appropriate channel. It should not be presented as a response to an open call unless a current official call is confirmed.

### 6.3 Finextra Expert Opinion

Finextra offers the strongest global fintech practitioner readership among the non-journal alternatives. Its community permits bona fide financial-technology professionals to publish expert opinion and integrates user-generated commentary with its financial-news platform.

Community information: <https://www.finextra.com/featurearticle/926/join-the-finextra-community>

The Finextra adaptation should emphasise:

- The gap between origination technology and collection architecture.
- The responsible use of behavioural data and artificial intelligence.
- Continuous monitoring and early intervention.
- The operational relationship between underwriting, servicing, restructuring, and recovery.
- The relevance of the framework beyond Kenya.

The global version may retain Kenyan data as a concrete case while explaining which principles apply more broadly.

### 6.4 LinkedIn Newsletter

A LinkedIn Newsletter offers the fastest route to publication, professional discussion, and audience building. LinkedIn sends email, push, and in-app notifications to newsletter subscribers. This makes it particularly suitable if *Underwrite for Collection* will initiate a continuing series on lifecycle credit architecture, responsible AI, collections, structured finance, or embedded finance.

Official guidance: <https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a522525/linkedin-newsletters>

The newsletter route should be used when immediate practitioner reach is more important than preserving unpublished status for a restrictive journal. The article can be supported by:

- A strong cover image.
- A short introductory post.
- The central architectural question at the beginning.
- A link to the full PDF or another permitted canonical version.
- A final question inviting perspectives from credit, banking, fintech, collections, accounting, regulation, and data-science professionals.

If the journal submission remains active, LinkedIn will carry only the short synopsis and selected insights. If the journal route is no longer being pursued, the complete practitioner article may be published as a newsletter edition.

### 6.5 SSRN

SSRN is best treated as a discoverable working-paper repository rather than a peer-reviewed journal. It can provide a persistent academic-style record and distribute the paper through relevant research networks.

Submission information: <https://www.elsevier.support/ssrn/answer/how-do-papers-get-on-ssrn>

Before SSRN submission, the paper should contain:

- An abstract.
- Keywords.
- Author affiliation or an accurate independent-author designation.
- A methodology section.
- A practical or numerical demonstration.
- Limitations.
- A complete reference list.
- A version date and appropriate disclosures.

SSRN publication must also be checked against the prior-publication and preprint policy of any journal being considered. Uploading to a repository is not automatically compatible with every journal.

## 7. Decision sequence

The publication decision will follow this order:

### Route A: Formal journal priority

1. Keep the complete manuscript unpublished on LinkedIn.
2. Prepare the journal-specific Word manuscript.
3. Add the worked example and journal-required sections.
4. Complete reference and factual verification.
5. Submit exclusively to the *Journal of Risk Management in Financial Institutions*.
6. Use LinkedIn only for a short teaser while the paper is under review.
7. If accepted, follow the publication agreement when sharing the article.
8. If declined, decide whether to revise for another formal outlet or release the practitioner version.

### Route B: Immediate industry reach

1. Publish the article as a LinkedIn Newsletter edition.
2. Share the accompanying LinkedIn post and PDF where appropriate.
3. Adapt the article for KIB or Finextra rather than resubmitting an identical copy without disclosure.
4. Disclose the earlier LinkedIn version to every prospective editor.
5. Develop a more empirical working paper for KBA or SSRN.

### Route C: Full LinkedIn article already public

1. Preserve the published URL and publication date.
2. Build a substantially extended journal manuscript.
3. Add empirical or synthetic evidence, methodology, results, limitations, and new implications.
4. Acknowledge the practitioner version in the manuscript where appropriate.
5. Send a pre-submission enquiry to the journal editor with the URL and proposed extension.
6. Proceed only after receiving written guidance.

## 8. Version control and rights management

Separate files will be maintained for:

- The canonical practitioner article.
- The LinkedIn edition.
- The journal manuscript.
- The KIB or Finextra adaptation.
- The KBA or SSRN working paper.

Each version will record its purpose, date, status, and relationship to earlier material. A simple publication ledger will track:

- Version title.
- Destination.
- Submission or publication date.
- Public URL.
- Exclusivity status.
- Copyright or licence terms.
- Permitted self-archiving version.
- Required acknowledgements.
- Editorial decision.

The author will not transfer rights to the same version inconsistently across outlets. Publisher-formatted files will not be reposted unless the agreement permits it. Accepted manuscripts, abstracts, and links will be shared only under the applicable terms.

## 9. Quality assurance

Before submission, the journal manuscript will be checked for:

- A word count between 2,500 and 6,000 words.
- An abstract no longer than 300 words.
- No more than seven keywords.
- Predominantly third-person professional style.
- A clear contribution and implications for business practice.
- A credible worked example, case study, or empirical illustration.
- Accurate Kenyan data and appropriately qualified comparisons.
- Clear separation of origination flows, outstanding NPL stocks, denominators, and reporting periods.
- Consistent treatment of behavioural scoring, artificial intelligence, policy action, IFRS 9, economic capital, and recovery.
- Accurate use of ring-fenced special-purpose-vehicle terminology.
- Complete and sequential Vancouver references.
- An accurate AI-use declaration.
- Editable and legible figures and tables.
- No unresolved citations, broken links, unsupported guarantees, or unlabelled illustrative results.
- Disclosure of all related public versions.
- Compliance with exclusivity and simultaneous-submission restrictions.
- A clean Word rendering with no overflow, clipped equations, or malformed characters.

## 10. Definition of done

The publication plan will be considered implemented when:

1. The existing practitioner article remains preserved.
2. A separate journal manuscript has been prepared in Word.
3. The manuscript meets the journal's length, abstract, keyword, style, citation, biography, figure, and disclosure requirements.
4. The practical or numerical demonstration has been added and validated.
5. Every factual statement and reference has been checked.
6. Any LinkedIn or other public version has been disclosed accurately.
7. The journal submission has been sent exclusively to the correct publisher address.
8. LinkedIn activity during review is limited to the permitted synopsis and selected insights.
9. A documented contingency route exists for KIB, KBA, Finextra, LinkedIn Newsletter, and SSRN.
10. Copyright, reuse, and self-archiving obligations are recorded before any later republication.

The locked strategic recommendation is therefore:

> Submit to the *Journal of Risk Management in Financial Institutions* first. If it is not accepted, or if journal exclusivity is no longer preferred, publish the practitioner edition through a LinkedIn Newsletter and then adapt it transparently for the Kenya Institute of Bankers or Finextra. Develop a more empirical version for the Kenya Bankers Association or SSRN.

This approach creates a genuine opportunity for peer-reviewed recognition while preserving the paper's ability to reach the practitioners whose decisions it is intended to influence.
