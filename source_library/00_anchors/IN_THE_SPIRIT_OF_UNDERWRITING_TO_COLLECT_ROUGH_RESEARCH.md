📊 RCT Design: Cash-Flow Data vs. Psychometric Testing

To achieve the "underwriting to collect" objective, the RCT should test
two distinct experimental treatments against a traditional control
group. This ensures you collect empirical data on which underwriting
mechanism predicts repayment and cash-flow alignment best.

**➡️ Treatment A: Cash-Flow Adjusted Underwriting**

- **The Hypothesis:** Underwriting based on real-time transaction
  velocity reduces default more than static asset collateral.

- **Data Sources:** Till numbers (M-Pesa Buy Goods/Pochi la Biashara),
  digital supply chain invoices, and bank statement e-vouchers.

- **Testing "Underwriting to Collect":** Randomize repayment
  frequencies. Test daily micro-deductions directly from merchant till
  balances versus weekly standard mobile transfers to find the
  friction-free collection sweet spot.

**➡️ Treatment B: Psychometric Underwriting**

- **The Hypothesis:** Psychological traits like integrity, risk
  aversion, and business acumen correlate with high repayment discipline
  in informal sectors.

- **Data Sources:** Gamified 20-minute digital assessments completed via
  smartphone or USSD during loan application.

- **Testing "Underwriting to Collect":** Group borrowers by psychometric
  profiles. Randomly assign different nudges (e.g., SMS reminders,
  automated voice calls, interactive WhatsApp bots) to see which
  communication style triggers collection responses for specific
  personality profiles.

**⚠️ Control Group: Traditional Scorecard**

- **The Process:** Standard credit bureau (CRB) check and basic manual
  business assessment.

- **The Purpose:** Acts as the baseline to measure whether alternative
  underwriting approaches yield lower Non-Performing Loan (NPL) rates
  and higher collection efficiency.

🛠️ CBK Regulatory Sandbox Application Requirements

The Central Bank of Kenya supervises financial innovations through its
regulatory sandbox framework to balance market growth with consumer
safety. To run an RCT safely, your application must meet strict
operational boundaries.

**📋 1. Core Eligibility Criteria**

- **Genuine Innovation:** You must prove the model offers a completely
  new utility or significantly improves efficiency for MSMEs compared to
  existing market solutions.

- **Intention to Deploy:** The product cannot be a pure academic
  exercise. You must demonstrate a clear intent to scale the model
  within Kenya's banking sector post-sandbox.

- **Consumer Benefit:** The application must highlight how the model
  reduces the cost of credit, expands financial inclusion, or solves the
  "lack of collateral" barrier.

**🛠️ 2. Boundary Controls & Risk Mitigation**

- **Trial Cohort Limits:** You must propose a strict limit on the number
  of MSMEs participating in the RCT (e.g., capped at 5,000 businesses)
  and maximum exposure ceilings (e.g., individual loans capped at KES
  50,000).

- **Guaranteed Safekeeping:** You must demonstrate adequate capital or
  financial guarantees to absorb potential losses during the trial,
  ensuring depositors or retail capital are not exposed.

- **The "Kill Switch":** Provide a clear, immediate exit strategy to
  wind down operations safely if NPL rates breach a predetermined
  catastrophic threshold (e.g., \>15%).

**🔒 3. Legal and Regulatory Compliance**

- **Data Protection Act (2019):** Because psychometric testing and
  cash-flow scraping harvest deep personal data, you must provide proof
  of explicit, unambiguous consent mechanisms. Data must be hosted in
  compliance with Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)
  guidelines.

- **Risk-Based Pricing:** CBK requires approval for interest rates. For
  the sandbox, you must justify your pricing model to ensure the RCT
  treatments do not unfairly penalize high-risk groups with predatory
  interest rates.

- **AML/CFT Frameworks:** Standard KYC compliance remains mandatory. The
  sandbox relaxes the *credit assessment rules*, but it does not relax
  Anti-Money Laundering laws.

📊 How the Sandbox RCT Powers a Causal Engine

A causal scoring engine requires experimental variations (interventions)
to map out precise cause-and-effect relationships rather than relying on
historical coincidences.
\[[<u>1</u>](https://firm.fm/en/paper/causal-ai-introduction-and-applications-in-risk-management-and-finance/),
[<u>2</u>](https://medium.com/@pemigffws/whitepaper-1-10bd1c1b77af)\]

**➡️ 1. Isolating Treatment Effects (ATE & CATE)**

- **The Problem:** In observational data, lenders do not know if a
  business repaid because they were inherently low-risk, or because the
  specific payment structure made it easy.

- **The Causal Solution:** By randomizing repayment terms (e.g., daily
  cash-flow deductions vs. weekly lump sums), the engine can isolate the
  **Average Treatment Effect (ATE)** of the collection mechanism itself.
  It explicitly isolates how the *intervention* alters the probability
  of default.
  \[[<u>1</u>](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/causal-inference-credit-scoring-unlocking-deeper-insights-sifuna-9fecf),
  [<u>2</u>](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9091/14/2/38)\]

**➡️ 2. Eliminating Confounding Biases**

- **The Problem:** Traditional alternative data scoring is prone to
  "spurious correlation" (e.g., assuming an MSME with low smartphone
  battery life is high-risk, when it may simply mean they operate in an
  area with frequent power outages).
  \[[<u>1</u>](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9091/14/2/38),
  [<u>2</u>](https://medium.com/@pemigffws/what-is-causal-ai-and-why-it-matters-for-lenders-bd32294a26fc)\]

- **The Causal Solution:** The sandbox RCT creates a controlled
  environment where confounding factors (like geographic infrastructure)
  are balanced across groups, allowing the engine to mathematically
  prove if the cash-flow velocity or psychometric trait *causes* the
  change in repayment behavior.
  \[[<u>1</u>](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/causal-inference-credit-scoring-unlocking-deeper-insights-sifuna-9fecf),
  [<u>2</u>](https://medium.com/@pemigffws/what-is-causal-ai-and-why-it-matters-for-lenders-bd32294a26fc),
  [<u>3</u>](https://firm.fm/en/paper/causal-ai-introduction-and-applications-in-risk-management-and-finance/)\]

**➡️ 3. Counterfactual Simulations for "Underwriting to Collect"**

- **The Application:** Causal engines excel at answering counterfactual
  "What if?" scenarios (*"If we increase this specific merchant's
  micro-loan by KES 20,000, will the cash-flow stress cause a default,
  or will the capital injection cause growth?"*).

- **The Sandbox Value:** Running these simulations using data harvested
  from the RCT provides the empirical foundation the CBK requires to
  approve **Risk-Based Credit Pricing Models**, as you can
  scientifically justify the exact risk premium applied to each MSME
  segment.
  \[[<u>1</u>](https://www.facebook.com/standardkenya/posts/causal-credit-scoring-is-here-and-is-the-future/1515903940579404/),

🔎 Why Retrospective Engineering Fails a Causal Engine

When a platform looks backward at a bank’s historical loan book, it
suffers from two major structural data constraints:

**➡️ 1. Total Lack of Support (The Unobserved Counterfactual)**

Historical data only shows how borrowers behaved under the bank's *old
rules* (e.g., rigid monthly repayments and collateral requirements). If
a bank rejected 40% of MSME applicants in the past, **there is zero data
on how those rejected applicants would have performed.** A causal AI
cannot accurately simulate an intervention on a population it has never
seen a response from.

**➡️ 2. Data Drift and Structural Breaks**

Macroeconomic landscapes mutate rapidly. A retrospective assessment
models human behavior based on old economic cycles. When fuel prices,
tax laws, or mobile money transaction behaviors shift, correlations
break down. “When external events disrupt the data rather than the
borrower, a model that cannot explain its own feature weights cannot
distinguish between the two,” meaning retrospective models risk blind
spots during sudden market shifts.
\[[<u>1</u>](https://pemig.co.ke/blog/what-implementing-alternative-borrower-assessment-actually-taught-us)\]

**➡️ 1. Neural Residualisation (The Confounder Filter)**

Alternative data streams (like raw M-Pesa transaction frequencies) are
full of messy, non-linear structural confounders (e.g., seasonal
holidays, general inflation).

- **How it works:** You run a neural network *only* to predict the
  target using known macro-confounders, and then subtract those
  predictions from the raw data.

- **The Benefit:** It isolates the "clean residual variance" of the
  borrower's true human behavior, passing clean, unconfounded inputs
  into the transparent statistical models below.

**➡️ 2. Horseshoe Prior (High-Dimensional Sparsity)**

When you capture psychometric data alongside hundreds of mobile phone
indicators, you face a "wide data" problem with massive noise.
\[[<u>1</u>](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/using-horseshoe-prior-in-hierarchical-model-for-variable-selection/26200),
[<u>2</u>](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22211)\]

- **How it works:** A global-local Horseshoe prior exerts heavy
  shrinkage, instantly pulling the coefficients of irrelevant noise
  variables to absolute zero.
  \[[<u>1</u>](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264999326000313),
  [<u>2</u>](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/using-horseshoe-prior-in-hierarchical-model-for-variable-selection/26200)\]

- **The Benefit:** It prevents overfitting while preserving the "heavy
  tails" of true, rare, high-impact predictive signals (like specific
  transaction frequencies or integrity traits).
  \[[<u>1</u>](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0264999326000313)\]

**➡️ 3. Hierarchical Proportional Cox Hazard Model with P-Splines**

Lenders shouldn't just model *if* a borrower defaults, but *when* they

- **How it works:** The Cox Proportional Hazard model tracks the
  time-to-event (default). By embedding **P-splines (Penalized
  Splines)**, you can map highly flexible, smooth, non-linear time
  trajectories for business risk without forcing the math into a rigid
  curve.

- **The Benefit:** It accounts for the variable cash-flow "breathing
  cycles" of informal markets (e.g., an agribusiness MSME that lacks
  cash in months 1–2 but peaks in month 3).

**➡️ 4. Hierarchical Bayesian Logistic Regression via Pólya-Gamma

➡️ 1. Contextual Multi-Arm Bandits (MAB) for Underwriting Exploration

Instead of standard random assignment, you deploy a **Contextual
Multi-Arm Bandit** utilizing **Thompson Sampling** or **Upper Confidence
Bound (UCB)** algorithms.

- **How it Works:** The "context" is the borrower's baseline data (e.g.,
  sector, initial transaction velocity, age). The "arms" are the
  different underwriting models setting the risk premium (K).

- **The Integration with Your Bayesian Stack:** Thompson Sampling
  requires a posterior distribution to draw from. Your **Hierarchical
  Bayesian Logistic Regression (via Pólya-Gamma augmentation)** outputs
  exactly that—a clean, continuously updated posterior distribution of
  repayment probability for each arm.

- **The Operational Value:** The bandit balances *exploration* (testing
  alternative models on new MSMEs to learn their true causal efficacy)
  with *exploitation* (routing incoming MSMEs to the underwriting model
  that has mathematically proven to minimize default for that specific
  sub-sector). If an arm is failing a specific demographic, the bandit
  organically reduces its exposure to near-zero without requiring manual
  programmatic intervention.

🔄 2. Reinforcement Learning & POMDPs for the Collection Lifecycle

Once the bandit selects an underwriting arm and a loan is disbursed, the
credit lifecycle transitions into a **Partially Observable Markov
Decision Process (POMDP)** to manage active collection ("Underwriting to
Collect").

**🔎 Why a POMDP?**

An MSME's true repayment intent and financial health are **hidden
states** (S). The bank cannot see the merchant's actual
cash-under-the-mattress or daily psychological stress. The bank can only
observe discrete **emitted signals/observations** (O), such as mobile
money till activity, app login frequencies, or delayed weekly payments.

**🛠️ The POMDP Architecture:**

- **States (S):** {Healthy Cash-Flow & High Intent, Stressed Cash-Flow
  but High Intent, Strategic Defaulter (Has cash but won't pay),
  Catastrophic Business Failure}.

- **Actions (A):** The active interventions the bank can take. {Do
  Nothing, Send Soft WhatsApp Nudge, Trigger Automated Voice Call, Offer
  Dynamic Term Extension, Temporarily Lower K, Disconnect Till
  Integration}.

- **Transitions (T) & Observations (O):** Your **Hierarchical Cox
  Proportional Hazard Model with P-splines** serves as the transition
  probability matrix. It calculates how time-to-default risk changes
  based on the action taken given the hidden state estimate.

- **Reward (R):** The reward function is explicitly mapped to maximize
  total net present value collected while minimizing collection costs
  and customer churn.

If you *only* use the challenger models to make passive, assistive

A causal engine requires active intervention to learn. If the challenger

(K), it cannot see how a borrower reacts to dynamic POMDP collection
nudges, and it cannot observe the counterfactual reality. It would slide
back into the trap of retrospective engineering.

To solve this, the sandbox portfolio must be structured into **two
distinct operational environments**: a live active environment and a
passive shadow environment.

🗺️ The Sandbox Portfolio Allocation Blueprint

sandbox structure separates the test portfolio into **Active
Interventions** (where the AI makes the final decision) and **Passive

**⚡ 1. The Active Sandbox Environment (10% to 20% of Portfolio)**

- **How it works:** In this ring-fenced silo, the challenger models are
  **fully autonomous** (acting as the "Maker") but are strictly governed
  by your **Maker-Checker human protocol** and safety constraints. When
  an MSME falls into this cohort, the algorithm sets the actual risk
  premium (K), executes the high-frequency till deductions, and fires
  the collection nudges.

- **Why it is necessary:** This is the only way to capture **Average
  Treatment Effects (ATE)**. The Multi-Arm Bandit must be allowed to
  allocate real capital and experience real defaults to continuously
  update its **Hierarchical Pólya-Gamma posterior distributions**.

- **The CBK Safe Boundary:** To protect the bank, this active pool is
  capped heavily (e.g., restricted to a maximum aggregate exposure of
  KES 50 Million or limited to 2,000 specific merchant accounts).

**👤 2. The Passive Shadow Environment (10% to 20% of Portfolio)**

- **How it works:** This is where the models are purely **assistive**.
  When an MSME applies, the legacy Champion model makes the final
  decision, issues the standard interest rate, and dictates traditional
  monthly collections. In the background, the Challenger models generate
  a "ghost prediction" and log what they *would* have done.

- **Why it is necessary:** This environment serves as the direct
  mathematical control group. It allows you to run your **Cox
  Proportional Hazard Splines** to evaluate if the challenger's ghost
  predictions are more accurate at predicting long-term default time
  horizons than the legacy model, without risking live bank capital.

**📊 Deconstructing the Analogies**

**➡️ 1. Roll Rates & Transition Matrices \\\approx \\ Social Mobility &
Life-Course Trajectories**

In banking, **Roll Rates** measure the percentage of borrowers moving
from one delinquency bucket to the next (e.g., from 30 days overdue to
60 days overdue) \[2.1\].

- **The Analogy:** In sociology, this is identical to **Social Mobility
  Matrices** or **Life-Course Analysis**, which track how individuals
  migrate between socioeconomic classes or life states (e.g., employment
  to long-term unemployment) over time.

- **The Causal Application:** Instead of treating roll rates as a
  static, inevitable downward slide, your **Pólya-Gamma Hierarchical
  Regression** treats them as dynamic human pathways that can be
  actively altered by timely bank interventions.

**➡️ 2. Exposure at Default (EAD) \\\approx \\ Pathogen Viral Load at
Onset**

**EAD** calculates the exact gross dollar amount the bank is exposed to
the moment a borrower defaults. For an MSME with an active revolving
line of credit, this fluctuates daily.

- **The Analogy:** This mirrors an individual's **Viral Load** at the
  moment an infection manifests. A higher exposure/load drastically
  increases the severity of the outcome.

- **The Causal Application:** Your **POMDP collection engine** acts as
  an antiviral treatment. By tracking high-frequency M-Pesa till
  velocities, the engine predicts an impending default state and
  automatically scales down the revolving credit limit *before* the
  crash happens—effectively lowering the EAD (viral load) dynamically.

**➡️ 3. Credit Contagion \\\approx \\ Epidemiological Disease
Transmission**

In credit risk, **Contagion** describes how the default of one major
market entity or sector cascades through the economy, causing otherwise
healthy businesses to default in a domino effect.

- **The Analogy:** This is the exact definition of an **Infectious
  Disease Outbreak (R₀ value)** in public health.

- **The Causal Application:** Informal Kenyan markets (like Gikomba or
  Kamukunji) are hyper-connected social clusters. If a primary wholesale
  supplier defaults, they starve their sub-retailers of stock. By
  modeling your sandbox RCT at the *cluster level* rather than the
  *individual account level*, your engine can identify systemic
  "super-spreaders" of risk and prioritize liquidity forbearance to them
  to insulate the broader market.

**➡️ 4. Vintage Analysis \\\approx \\ Birth Cohort Longitudinal
Studies**

Banks use **Vintage Analysis** to track the performance of a group of
loans originated in the exact same month/quarter across their entire
lifecycle to spot underwriting flaws.

- **The Analogy:** In social science, this is a **Birth Cohort Study**
  (e.g., tracking children born in 2026 over 20 years to see how
  environmental variables affect life outcomes).

- **The Causal Application:** Your **Cox Proportional Hazard Splines**
  rely heavily on this. By treating each month’s MSME sandbox sign-ups
  as a distinct birth cohort, the engine can separate the natural
  degradation of a loan (aging) from external economic shocks (e.g., a
  sudden national fuel levy hike affecting that specific vintage).

**➡️ 5. Loss Given Default (LGD) \\\approx \\ Severity of Sequelae**

**LGD** measures the unrecoverable economic loss a bank suffers after
exhausting all collection efforts, expressed as a percentage of the EAD.

- **The Analogy:** In clinical research, this is the **Severity of
  Sequelae**—the permanent, long-term debilitating damage or disability
  left behind in a patient after the acute phase of a disease has
  passed.

- **The Causal Application:** A high LGD means the business was
  completely destroyed by the default process. Your actuarial framework
  views a low LGD not just as "recovering assets through collateral,"
  but as preserving the borrower’s underlying economic capacity so they
  can eventually step back into the financial ecosystem.
