# From KESONIA to Sustainable Collection

## Risk-Based Pricing, MSME Credit Quality and Lifecycle Underwriting in Kenya

**Author:** Neville Maloba  
**Email:** nevillemaloba@gmail.com  
**Proposed audience:** Kenya Bankers Association Research Centre, member banks, policymakers, credit-risk practitioners, digital lenders, and development-finance partners

### Research problem

Kenya's revised risk-based credit-pricing framework places KESONIA at the centre of variable-rate loan pricing. The reform improves the visibility of the market benchmark and separates it from the bank-specific pricing premium and disclosed fees. Its practical success, however, depends on more than transparent origination pricing. The lender must understand how the loan performs after disbursement, how quickly benchmark changes reach customer rates, when repayment capacity deteriorates, which interventions produce durable cure, and how realised cash outcomes affect expected credit loss, capital, funding, and the willingness to lend.

This study develops and tests a lifecycle view of underwriting. Underwriting begins at origination but continues through payment behaviour, utilisation, modification, cure, delinquency, default, recovery, and closure. Collection is therefore treated as a measurable design property of the credit product rather than a function activated only after arrears become severe.

### Primary question

How does Kenya's transition to KESONIA-anchored risk-based pricing affect pricing transmission, MSME credit access, and loan collectability, and how can a lifecycle-underwriting framework improve the connection between origination, early intervention, durable cure, and net recovery?

### Proposed evidence

The public-data layer will combine official KESONIA and CBR series, commercial-bank weighted average rates, KBA Total Cost of Credit disclosures, KBA MSME Credit Dashboard indicators, CBK Credit Officer Surveys, and official macroeconomic controls. It will describe benchmark transmission, bank-product pricing dispersion, credit conditions, and asset-quality patterns while respecting the different denominators used for lending flows, outstanding credit, and NPL stocks.

The preferred institution-data layer will use anonymised account or cohort histories covering price, schedule, payments, arrears transitions, cure, modification, intervention, write-off, recovery, and collection cost. It will compare an origination-only credit model with lifecycle hazard or multistate models. Hierarchical pooling and governed nonlinearities will be introduced where they improve out-of-vintage calibration and decision value. Advanced AI representations will be tested as incremental challengers rather than assumed to be necessary.

### Expected contribution

The study connects two policy conversations that are often separated: the transmission of a transparent reference rate into credit pricing and the management of credit after disbursement. It proposes a common measurement chain from benchmark and premium setting to payment performance, intervention, net recovery, IFRS 9, capital, and funding. It also distinguishes regulatory requirements, contractual terms, internal credit policy, predictive models, and authorised collection actions.

### Practical outputs

- A five-page KBA research proposal.
- A reproducible public-data pricing and credit-quality study.
- An institution-data lifecycle model, subject to partnership.
- A shorter Kenya Bankers Economic Bulletin article based on “Underwrite for Collection.”
- An implementation scorecard for pricing transmission, early warning, cure, re-default, net recovery, ECL, and capital.
- A policy brief for banks, KBA, CBK, data partners, and development-finance stakeholders.

### Current readiness

The conceptual architecture, Kenyan policy framing, mathematical foundations, and practitioner narrative are substantially developed. The next research tasks are to freeze the empirical specification, assemble the public dataset, obtain an institutional data partner, estimate the models, and subject the findings to independent technical and industry review.
