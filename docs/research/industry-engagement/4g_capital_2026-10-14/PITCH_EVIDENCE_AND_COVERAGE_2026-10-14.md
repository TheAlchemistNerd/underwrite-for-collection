## 1. Recommended proposition

Lead with the business cycle and the lender’s evidence: working-capital credit should be evaluated through the business activity it funds, settled cash, operating obligations, repayment, customer outcomes and the next viable stock cycle. Underwrite for Collection provides the central lifecycle and collection framework. Mwendo Pamoja contributes cash-flow modelling, Bayesian review decisions, interventions and financing economics.

The IFC Catalytic First Loss Guarantee partnership gives this proposal a concrete portfolio-finance setting. The application should connect merchant and loan-level evidence to eligibility, loss allocation, claim and cash dates, recovery sharing, portfolio reporting and commercial results. Public sources do not establish that a particular 4G product, including UPIA or KUZA, is included in the guaranteed pool. The 4G-specific SII mentions advisory support for a distributor-finance product; confirm product eligibility and all contractual rules from the executed agreement before modelling them as facts.

## 3. IFC partnership: exact public figures and their scopes

### IFC SII project 53511 — 4G-specific narrative

The IFC Summary of Investment Information identifies project 53511, “MFP-CFLG-4G CAP.” It was disclosed 24 May 2026, updated 4 July 2026, approved 29 June and signed 30 June 2026. The page, checked on 14 October 2026, displays “Pending Disbursement.” Thus it is accurate to say approved and signed; the public page does not show disbursement completed.

The narrative describes an **unfunded financial guarantee** between IFC and 4G supporting a new eligible Kenyan MSME loan portfolio of up to USD 44.4 million equivalent in Kenyan shillings, with focus on microenterprises and women-owned microenterprises. It describes:

- First-loss layer up to USD 2.2 million equivalent; IFC covers up to USD 2 million and 4G bears the remainder of that layer.
- A further USD 42.2 million layer, shared pari passu, with IFC exposure capped at USD 4.2 million and the remaining balance borne by 4G.
- IFC narrative maximum risk of USD 6.2 million, combining its two stated caps.
- The narrative’s minimum 21× “core mobilization” objective.
- IFC advisory support for guarantee onboarding, enhancement of underwriting methodology and a distributor-finance product.
- Public development objectives of improved access to MSME finance and demonstration of commercial viability for underserved segments.

The SII separately displays “IFC’s Investment as Approved by the Board” as **USD 8.20 million**, marked indicative, under the Guarantee product line. This differs from the USD 6.2 million maximum risk stated in the narrative. Preserve both as separate fields in a disclosure-reconciliation table. Do not add, substitute, or reinterpret the amounts without a contract or clarification from IFC/4G.

### IFC 4 August announcement — three-transaction aggregate

The IFC announcement says its first three African CFLG transactions are with 4G Capital, Equity Bank Kenya and KCB Bank Kenya. Across those three transactions, IFC reports USD 24.2 million committed, supported by USD 11 million in IDA Private Sector Window financing; approximately USD 144.4 million equivalent in local-currency lending; approximately USD 120.2 million additional lending; and an **11:1 target leverage ratio**. These are programme/three-transaction figures, not 4G-only amounts.

The 4G announcement dated 5 August repeats the same aggregate figures. The project-specific SII’s minimum 21× core mobilization figure and the announcement’s 11:1 aggregate target have different labels and scope. The public documents do not establish matching denominators or a reconciliation. Show them separately; do not present either as a conversion of the other.

### What the public disclosure does not provide

The executed guarantee agreement, final eligibility schedule, guarantee fee, portfolio inclusion rules, currency conversion method, claims evidence and deadlines, waiting period, recovery-sharing mechanics, reinstatement, amendments, termination and dispute process are not set out in full in the public SII. A reproducible worked case should use explicit placeholders until 4G and IFC confirm them. In particular, it should not infer that UPIA or KUZA loan exposures qualify because the SII mentions an overall portfolio or distributor finance advisory.

## 4. How to connect the guarantee to Underwrite for Collection

Build a dated loan/claim/settlement ledger with one entity-resolved key linking merchant, distributor, order, facility, payment, claim and recovery events. For each exposure, preserve eligibility status and the source field supporting it. Carry the business cash cycle separately from the protection transaction:

**Business:** credit → goods ordered → delivery verified → inventory sold → receipts settled → operating and household needs → repayment → next stock cycle.

**Guarantee:** eligible exposure → defined eligible loss → claim prepared → claim submitted → acceptance/dispute → guarantee cash → subsequent recovery → contractual remittance.

The guarantee changes the lender’s loss allocation and, subject to claim timing, may change ultimate net cash. It does not itself improve merchant turnover, create eligible cash or remove claim lag. The study should report borrower outcomes, lender retained risk, guarantee payments, fees, funding/liquidity needs and recoveries separately. With and without protection comparisons should use the same borrower and trading scenarios before evaluating portfolio growth.

The illustrative tranche equations already in the 4G review are appropriate only as a teaching structure, not as the actual 4G waterfall. A live model needs contract-specific attachment, sharing order, caps, definitions, currency, timing and post-claim recovery rules. Store an accounting claim receivable separately from expected settlement cash; fund the settlement lag explicitly.

## 5. Bayesian and causal decision contribution

Use hierarchical estimates to structure evidence across merchant type, geography/branch, product, distributor, vintage and repeat cycle, with appropriate pooling for sparse groups. Decision records should present posterior credible intervals, predictive cash outcomes, data-quality and maturity flags, and the posterior probability of crossing an approved limit/affordability threshold. A credible interval for a parameter, a predictive interval for future cash, and an estimated causal effect of a support intervention answer different questions.

For support training or field assistance, predefine the service being varied, comparison condition, assignment unit, customer protections, outcome window, branch/distributor spillovers, and primary business and collection outcomes. Suitable outcomes include sales/inventory conversion, gross margin, cash after operating needs, repayment timing, durable cure, net collections, customer feedback and cost of support. A contextual bandit may be a later allocation method for approved support choices after safe policies, randomised or otherwise defensible evidence, monitoring and review routes exist. Sequential RL does not belong in the initial guarantee ledger merely because a risk-sharing agreement exists.

## 6. Kenya legal framework checked for the pitch

### Central Bank of Kenya (Non-Deposit Taking Credit Providers) Regulations, 2026, Legal Notice 191

The official Kenya Law text is saved in the project source archive. Gazette publication is 29 September 2026. Under section 27(1) of the Interpretation and General Provisions Act, subsidiary legislation comes into operation on publication unless another date is provided. LN 191 has no separate commencement date in its text. Regulation 96(3)–(4) says a provider licensed under the 2022 Digital Credit Providers Regulations is not subject to the new registration requirement and continues as if licensed under LN 191. CBK’s September 2026 directory lists Fourth Generation Capital Limited as licensed on 27 March 2023. The transition is therefore continuity of licence, with the new operating requirements to be considered from the instrument’s operation; it does not support telling 4G to submit a fresh application solely because the rules changed.

Requirements material to a proposed evidence, pricing and AI pilot include:

| Regulation | Textual obligation | Design implication for proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 24–25, provision of credit and credit policy | Credit provision follows the provider’s policy and applicable law; maintain a suitable credit policy. | Map each proposed output to an existing policy/decision right, define any governance approvals and preserve the actual offer and accepted agreement. |
| 26, products | New products or variation of existing product features, including interest rates, require prior written CBK approval; customer notice at least 30 days before a variation takes effect. | Treat any new offer, altered rate or product feature as a formal product-governance question. A research model output alone does not change a product. |
| 58, consent to process data | Requires lawful, fair and transparent processing; sets out separate express consent-form and notice contents for customer personal data processed by an NDTCP. | The pilot data inventory must map purpose, data categories, accessors, retention and consent/other applicable legal requirements. Preserve the statutory wording and evaluate it alongside the Data Protection Act/Regulations for the concrete data flow. |
| 59, pricing parameters | Written pricing model must identify cost of funds, capital, operations, shareholder return, risk premium, fees/charges; justify costs; consider customer risk/credit behaviour and CRB scores; provide all-inclusive pricing (subject to stated third-party-cost exception) and APR disclosure. | Strong specification anchor for a transparent price bridge: expected loss, cost, funding, capital, operations, fees and approved margin each have explicit owners and evidence. |
| 60, AI | When AI is used for lending decisions, requires explanation of affected automated decisions, disclosure when interacting with AI rather than a human agent, human oversight/review, bias/unfair-outcome risk assessment, technical robustness/accuracy, transparency/accountability/explainability, and privacy/security controls. | A proposed Bayesian or AI output should be a reviewable decision aid, with uncertainty, reason/evidence, model version, reviewer, outcome and complaint/correction routes. Do not treat the research as a licensed production decision service. |
| 61, cybersecurity | Requires appropriate technical/operational cyber standards and controls over customer data and systems. | Minimise and pseudonymise the research extract, define access and event security, and keep bank/system connection as a scoped future integration. |
| 96, transition | Existing 2022-licensed NDTCPs continue and are deemed licensed under the new Regulations. | Record the 2022 CBK licence continuity; do not imply a new application is required. |

The Consumer Protection Act, Data Protection Act 2019, Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021 and any current CBK codes/directives remain relevant to the product and data design. This pitch note identifies the specific new LN 191 anchors above; a proposed pilot needs a purpose-specific legal and compliance review by the responsible entities.
