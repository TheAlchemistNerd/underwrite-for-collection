Part I – PRELIMINARY
1. Citation.
These Regulations may be cited as the Central Bank of Kenya (Non-Deposit Taking Credit Providers)
Regulations, 2026.
2. Interpretation
In these Regulations, unless the context otherwise requires—
“Act” means the Central Bank of Kenya Act (Cap. 491);
“associate” means—
(a) in relation to a non-deposit taking credit provider or other body corporate means—
(i) its holding company or its subsidiary;
(ii) a holding company of its subsidiary;
(iii) its non-operating holding company as its subsidiary;
(iv) a subsidiary of a non-operating holding company;
(v) any person who controls the company or body corporate whether alone or with his associates
or with its associates; or
(b) in relation to an individual, means any—
(i) member of his family; and
(ii) company or other body corporate controlled directly or indirectly, by him whether alone or
with his associates;
“Bank” means the Central Bank of Kenya;
“beneﬁcial owner” has the meaning assigned to it under the Companies Act (Cap. 486);
“borrower” means a customer or a consumer;
“certiﬁcate of registration” means a document issued by the Bank to a person who has applied for and
fulﬁls the requirements for registration under the Act and these Regulations;
“charges” includes all the payments that a customer makes, is required to make, or agrees to make to a
non-deposit taking credit provider in consideration of the loan by the non-deposit taking credit provider
to the customer, and all interest, fees, expenses and costs associated with the provision of the loan;

“non-performing loan” has the meaning assigned to it in regulation 57 of these Regulations;
“pay as you go” means a credit arrangement where a consumer acquires access to the use of an asset by
making partial or periodic payments with the ultimate intention to own the asset, and the provider retains
the right to restrict, deactivate, or repossess the asset in the event of non-payment by the consumer;
“place of business” means any premise including the head ofﬁce, branch, or any such premises as may be
approved by the Bank where the non-deposit taking credit provider carries on business;
“reckless” includes—
(a) offering a loan or loan product contrary to the Act, these Regulations, the code of conduct or any
directives issued by the Bank; or
(b) failing to observe the non-deposit taking credit provider’s own policies as approved by the board of
directors;
“senior ofﬁcer” means a person who has ultimate responsibility for managing a speciﬁc function or
business area of a non-deposit taking credit provider including ﬁnance, compliance, risk, information
technology, credit, debt recovery, marketing, or any other similar function as may be speciﬁed by the
Bank;
“signiﬁcant shareholder” means a person other than the Government or a public entity, who holds directly
or indirectly or otherwise has a beneﬁcial interest in ten percent or more of the share capital of a non-

3. Application.
(1) These Regulations shall apply to non-deposit taking credit business not regulated under any other
written law.
(2) These Regulations shall apply to any person seeking to conduct non-deposit taking credit business
unless that person has been licensed or registered by the Bank under this Act or is permitted to do
so under any other written law.
(3) These Regulations shall not apply to—
(a) an institution licensed under the Banking Act (Cap. 488);
(b) a credit guarantee company or credit guarantee provider regulated under the Central Bank of
Kenya Act (Cap. 491);
(c) an institution licensed under the Microﬁnance Act (Cap. 493C);
(d) a co-operative society registered under the Co-operative Societies Act (Cap. 490);
(e) a Sacco society licensed under the Sacco Societies Act (Cap. 490B);
(f) credit arrangements involving the provision of credit by a person that is merely incidental
to the sale of goods or provision of services by the person whose primary business is the
provision of the goods or services;
(g) lease ﬁnance transactions in Kenya regulated under any written law;
(h) hire-purchase arrangements involving the bailment of goods under the Hire Purchase Act
(Cap. 507);
(i) a person whose non-deposit taking credit business is regulated under any other written law;
or
(j) any other person approved by the Bank and whose business does not fall under the deﬁnition
of “ﬁnancial institution” under the Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act (Cap.
59A).
4. Name approval.
(1) Promoters of a proposed non-deposit taking credit provider shall propose at least three company
names with the Business Registration Service.
(2) The promoters shall submit the proposed three company names to the Bank for approval, in order
of preference, together with the following supporting information—
(a) name search and reservation document obtained from Business Registration Service;
(b) names and addresses of the proposed shareholders, including certiﬁed copies of requisite
identiﬁcation documents or certiﬁcates of incorporation or registration in case of corporate
entities; and
(c) a high-level brief providing information on the proposed business including—
(i) company proﬁle;
(ii) nature of proposed business indicating the proposed business activities;
(iii) evidence of availability and amount of capital;

(v) organizational structure describing proposed functional units through which the

(10) Where the promoters fail to comply with paragraph (8), the approval of the proposed name shall
lapse and the promoters shall be required to cease using the proposed name.
(11) Without prejudice to paragraph (9), a promoter issued with approval to use any name by the Bank
shall not transact a non-deposit taking credit business or misrepresent themselves as having
authority to carry on non-deposit taking credit business.

Part II – LICENSING OF NON-DEPOSIT TAKING CREDIT PROVIDERS
5. Prohibition from carrying on business without a licence or registration
A person shall not establish a non-deposit taking credit business in Kenya or otherwise hold themself out
as carrying on non-deposit taking credit business unless that person is licensed or registered by the Bank
in accordance with the Act and these Regulations or is a person whose non-deposit taking credit business
is regulated under any other written law.
6. Application for licence.
(1) A person who seeks to establish a non-deposit taking credit business in Kenya and whose initial
capital is not less than twenty million shillings shall apply to the Bank for a licence using Form 1 set
out in the First Schedule.
(2) An application under paragraph (1) shall be accompanied by—
(a) a certiﬁed copy of the certiﬁcate of incorporation of the applicant issued under the
Companies Act (Cap. 486);
(b) a certiﬁed copy of the Memorandum and Articles of Association of the applicant;
(c) a certiﬁed copy of the certiﬁcate of registration of a Business Name issued by the Business
Registration Service where the applicant intends to carry on business under a business name
which is not on the certiﬁed copy of the certiﬁcate of incorporation;
(d) notiﬁcation of the applicant’s registered address;
(e) a certiﬁcate issued pursuant to section 19 of the Data Protection Act (Cap. 411C);
(f) a statement on how the applicant shall comply with the provisions of Part VII of the
Consumer Protection Act (Cap. 501);
(g) a statement on how the applicant shall comply with the provisions of Part VI of the
Competition Act (Cap. 504);
(h) evidence of availability and amount of capital to be invested in the proposed business;
(i) information on the source of capital;
(j) a certiﬁed copy of a certiﬁcate of incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association of
any corporate body that has a signiﬁcant shareholding in the applicant;
(k) a description of any information and communication technology system to be used;
(l) a description of delivery channels or platforms to be used in conducting the business or
delivering services;
(m) a description of, and terms and conditions of, credit products and services which the
applicant intends to provide;
(n) draft agreement with a service provider in relation to provision of delivery channel, where
applicable;

7. Issuance of a licence.
(1) In assessing the application, the Bank shall consider the following—
(a) adequacy of the information and documents furnished in support of the application;

(b) the professional and moral suitability of the persons proposed to manage or control the non-

(c) the sources and evidence of funds to be invested by or in the non-deposit taking credit

(d) the public interest to be served by issuing the licence.
(2) The Bank may, if satisﬁed that the applicant meets the requirements, grant with or without

pay double the annual fees if it is paid within three months from 31st day of December of the year
when the fees fall due.
(6) If the annual fees are not paid in accordance with the requirements of paragraph (5), the Bank may
revoke the licence.
8. Discontinuation of licence application.
(1) Where an applicant has not responded to a request by the Bank for additional information for a
period of three months, the Bank may discontinue the processing of the licence application.
(2) After the expiry of the three months referred to in paragraph (1), the Bank may issue a fourteen
days’ notice to show cause to the applicant why the licence application processing should not be
discontinued and the Bank may consider any representations made pursuant to the notice to show
cause.
(3) The Bank shall notify the applicant of the decision on discontinuation of processing of the licence
application within seven days of receipt of the representations, or such longer time as may be
necessary.
(4) Where an application has been discontinued under paragraph (1), the applicant may make a fresh
application for a licence on such terms as the Bank may impose.

(2) Where a non-deposit taking credit provider processes the personal data of a customer, the non-
deposit taking credit provider shall ensure that the consent from a customer is—
(a) express, freely given, speciﬁc and unambiguous;
(b) separately obtained through a consent form developed by the non-deposit taking credit
provider which should not form part of or be incorporated in the letter or offer executed or
terms and conditions accepted by the customer;
(c) capable of being withdrawn by the customer; and
(d) veriﬁable and forms part of the record of the customer’s information; and
(3) A non-deposit taking credit provider shall inform the customer—
(a) of the types of personal data which will be processed;
(b) who may have access to the personal data; and
(c) of the duration of retention of the customer’s personal data.
(4) Subject to paragraph (3)—
(a) a privacy notice, or general notice of a similar nature shall not satisfy the obligation by
a non-deposit taking credit provider to give their express consent as required under this
Regulation; and
(b) the information in paragraph (3), shall form the minimum information contained in the
consent form to be signed or accepted by the customer.
(5) A non-deposit taking credit provider that fails to comply with paragraph (1) shall be subject to any
administrative action imposed under the Data Protection Act (Cap. 411C).
59. Pricing parameters.
(1) A non-deposit taking credit provider shall develop and implement a pricing model which outlines
clear pricing parameters.
(2) The pricing model shall comply with credit pricing principles and shall contain the—
(a) components of pricing clearly identiﬁed and outline each element incorporated including
cost of funds, cost of capital, cost of operations, return to shareholders, risk premium, and
other fees and charges;
(b) costs clearly identiﬁed and justiﬁed and which costs shall be directly related to lending and
any other costs which shall be supported and justiﬁed;
(c) risk proﬁle of customer which shall be taken into account through evaluation of each
individual customer’s credit behavior enabling a non-deposit taking credit provider to apply
differential interest rates based on a customer’s credit-worthiness;
(d) criteria for determining non-performance;

(e) credit reference bureau information through incorporation of credit reference bureau scores;
(f) all-inclusive pricing which shall include all other charges except third party costs; and
(g) full disclosure of the annual percentage rate to be charged to a borrower as applied on a loan.
60. Use of artiﬁcial intelligence.
A non-deposit taking credit shall, when using artiﬁcial intelligence for making lending decisions, ensure
that—
(a) automated decisions are explained to customers where they affect a loan application;
(b) customers are informed when they are interacting with an artiﬁcial intelligence system and not a
human agent;
(c) human oversight and review of artiﬁcial intelligence decisions and automated credit decisions is
retained;
(d) risk assessments are undertaken to identify and mitigate discriminatory bias and unfair outcomes
from data collected on the customers;
(e) artiﬁcial models deployed are technically robust, accurate, and capable of making correct
judgments;
(f) artiﬁcial intelligence algorithms, logic, data and design processes are transparent, accountable and
explainable; and
(g) data privacy and security concerns are adequately managed.
61. Compliance with cyber security standards.
(1) A non-deposit taking credit provider shall comply with appropriate technical and operational
cybersecurity standards.
(2) Without prejudice to paragraph (1), non-deposit taking credit provider shall—
(a) adopt the necessary infrastructure to monitor, detect, prevent, and respond to cyber threats
to the integrity of a customer’s data;
(b) ensure staff have the requisite skills and continually build capacity to identify sources of
cybercrime and respond effectively to any cyber threats; and
(c) develop authentication controls to protect customer data, transactions and systems.

Part VIII – CODE OF CONDUCT
70. Objectives of the code of conduct.
(1) The objective of the code of conduct is to—
(a) ensure sound conduct of non-deposit taking credit business;
(b) establish standards to be adhered to by non-deposit taking credit providers; and
(c) ensure fair treatment of consumers of non-deposit taking credit business services and the
public.
(2) A non-deposit taking credit provider shall have the responsibility to adopt appropriate processes
and procedures to meet the objectives of this Code.
(3) Without prejudice to paragraph (2), the Bank shall be notiﬁed of any change in the code of conduct,
at least thirty days, before being varied.
71. Guiding principles.
The relationship between a non-deposit taking provider and its customers shall be guided by the
principles of—
(a) fairness;
(b) transparency;
(c) accountability; and
(d) reliability.

decisions; and

(a) the violation did not take place;

95. Voluntary liquidation of business.
(1) A non-deposit taking credit provider may, with the approval of the Bank, voluntarily liquidate or
close its business if it is unable to meet all its liabilities.
(2) An application for the Bank’s approval for the purposes of paragraph (1) shall be in such form as
may be speciﬁed by the Bank.
(3) The Bank may, upon receipt of an application under paragraph (2), approve the application if
satisﬁed as to the solvency of the non-deposit taking credit provider.
(4) Where the Bank approves an application by a non-deposit taking credit provider in paragraph (3)
the non-deposit taking credit provider shall cease its operations.
(5) Without prejudice to paragraph (4), where activities are incidental to the realization, conservation
and preservation of its assets and settlement of its obligations, a non-deposit taking credit provider
shall continue with its operations subject to such directions as the Bank may issue.
(6) The Bank shall, upon approval of a voluntary liquidation or closure, ensure the smooth execution of
the liquidation process including recovery of any outstanding loans and discharge of liabilities.
(7) The Bank shall upon granting an approval for voluntary liquidation or closure, publish in the
Gazette a notice to the effect that the non-deposit taking credit provider is in the process of closing
its business and shall not engage in any new business activities other than recovery of its loans and
discharge of its liabilities.
(8) The Bank shall publish in the Gazette a notice to the effect that voluntary liquidation or closure, has
been completed and the licence or registration of the non-deposit taking credit provider has been
revoked.

SECOND SCHEDULE [r. 14(1), 22(8), 74(d)]
CRITERIA
Part A – CRITERIA FOR DETERMINING PROFESSIONAL AND MORAL SUITABILITY
(1) For the purposes of determining the professional and moral suitability of persons, proposed to be directors
and senior ofﬁcers of a non-deposit taking credit provider, the Bank shall have regard to the following
qualities, in so far as they are reasonably determinable—
(a) possession of relevant qualiﬁcation, knowledge, skills and experience;
(b) ability to recommend and implement sound practices based on previous business experience;
57

--- PDF page 64 ---
The Central Bank of Kenya (Non-Deposit Taking Credit Providers) Regulations, 2026 (Legal Notice 191 of
2026)  Kenya
(c) ability to provide objective advice;
(d) ability to avoid conﬂicts of interest in activities and commitments with other organizations.
(2) Without prejudice to the generality of the provisions of paragraph (1), the Bank, may have regard to the
present and previous conduct and activities of the person concerned in business or ﬁnancial matters and,
in particular, to any evidence that such person—
(a) has been convicted of an offence of fraud or any other offence of which dishonesty is an element;
(b) has contravened the provisions of any law designed for the protection of members of the public
against ﬁnancial loss due to the dishonesty or incompetence of or malpractices;
(c) was a director or a senior ofﬁcer of an institution that has been liquidated or is under liquidation or
statutory management;
(d) has taken part in any business practices that, in the opinion of the Bank, were fraudulent,
prejudicial or otherwise improper (whether unlawful or not);
(e) has taken part in or been associated with any other business practices as would, or has otherwise
conducted himself in such manner as to cast doubt on his competence and soundness of judgment;
(f) has defaulted in the repayment of a loan for three consecutive months advanced by a licensed
ﬁnancial institution; and
(g) has been adjudged bankrupt.
Part B – CRITERIA FOR DETERMINING MORAL
SUITABILITY OF SIGNIFICANT SHAREHOLDERS
(1) For the purposes of determining the moral suitability of signiﬁcant shareholders of a non-deposit taking
credit provider, the Bank shall have regard to the previous conduct and activities of the signiﬁcant
shareholder concerned in business or ﬁnancial matters and, in particular, to any evidence that such person
—
(a) has been convicted of the offence of fraud or any other offence of which dishonesty is an element;
and
(b) has contravened the provisions of any law designed for the protection of members of the public
against ﬁnancial loss due to the dishonesty or malpractices by persons engaged in the provision of
banking, insurance, investment or other ﬁnancial services.
(2) Where the signiﬁcant shareholder is a corporate entity, its directors and senior ofﬁcers shall satisfy the
criterion speciﬁed in paragraph (a) above.
(3) The Bank may request any person or corporate entity to furnish such information as may be necessary in
determining the moral suitability of signiﬁcant shareholders.
