# Moniepoint and Underwrite for Collection reproducible cash flow case

## Purpose and research questions

This independent proposed application follows a synthetic merchant from source transactions to a reviewed facility, received repayments and lender contribution. Its foundation is *Underwrite for Collection*, particularly the mature treatment of economic identity, cash provenance, temporal evidence, policy boundaries, controlled deployment and realised cash. The case uses KES and author-selected assumptions to illustrate a Kenyan application of cash-flow lending. Moniepoint's published working-capital proposition supplies industry context; the exported records are generated by the accompanying Python model. [1], [2]

Three research questions organise the case. First, how do transaction provenance, coverage and information timing change the evidence for an affordable facility? Second, how should uncertainty, a dated cash calendar and accountable review determine the amount and schedule? Third, how do subsequent settlement delays, operating pressure, data defects and servicing decisions change collected cash, borrower resources and lender contribution? These questions carry the same merchant and facility identifiers through the whole calculation.

## The complete evidence chain

Three historical months end at decision day 90. The merchant receives customer payments through a bank account and a wallet, mapped to one controlled enterprise. Seventy per cent of verified trade receipts passes through the bank and thirty per cent through the wallet. The owner is a separately identified economic entity. Each raw record contains an economic-event ID, account, source, settlement day, known-at day, classification-known day, permitted purpose and graph/classification versions. Separate truth records support synthetic verification; the decision function consumes only the evidence eligible at its cutoff.

The historical monthly receipts are KES405,000, KES415,000 and KES410,000. Each month also includes KES45,000 owner capital, KES30,000 financing and a KES15,000 correction credit with its matching debit. The final month's KES500,000 gross credits reconcile to KES410,000 operating receipts, KES45,000 capital support, KES30,000 borrowing and KES15,000 reversal credits. The repayment calculation carries operating receipts into capacity while retaining the other classes in the liquidity and audit views. Known existing contractual payments are included among obligations. The future case is conditional on those obligation amounts remaining the stated KES25,000 per month; an implemented bank integration must reconcile the full external-facility schedules at each review.

This implements Part 2a §2.1, “Resolve the economic entity before calculating cash-flow features”, and §4, “Reconstruct the state the decision could actually see”. Exact economic IDs remove a duplicate without inventing a second sale. A KES50,000 transfer between controlled accounts nets to zero. A missing wallet month fails the coverage gate. A receipt classification first available on day 96 remains unresolved in the day 90 snapshot and becomes usable at the later cutoff. This is a deterministic reconciliation fixture; probabilistic entity matching would add candidate links, confidence and reviewer outcomes to the same contract. [1]

The underlying cash bridge is:

C = 410,000 − 270,000 − 70,000 − 20,000 − 25,000 = KES25,000.

Here C is monthly cash remaining after supplier commitments, operating costs, household drawings and existing debt payments, before the proposed new facility. Historical residuals are KES20,000, KES30,000 and KES25,000. The KES35,000 requested monthly payment exceeds the last observed residual. That comparison leads into a quantified review of amount, uncertainty and payment dates.

## A small Bayesian calculation with an explicit decision use

The executable teaching model assumes conditionally independent monthly residuals Cₘ ∼ Normal(μ, σ²), with a fixed σ = KES8,000. The prior is μ ∼ Normal(m₀, s₀²), where m₀ = KES25,000 and s₀ = KES15,000. These are declared calibration assumptions, chosen to make the calculation auditable with three observations. The familiar Normal conjugate update is an accessible implementation of the distinction between parameter uncertainty and future cash variation. [3]

vₙ = (1/s₀² + n/σ²)⁻¹.

mₙ = vₙ(m₀/s₀² + ΣₘCₘ/σ²).

μ | C ∼ Normal(mₙ, vₙ).

Cₙ₊₁ | C ∼ Normal(mₙ, σ² + vₙ).

In these expressions n is the number of historical months, mₙ the posterior mean, vₙ its posterior variance, and Cₙ₊₁ next month's cash residual. Completing the square in the prior-times-likelihood exponent yields posterior precision 1/s₀² + n/σ² and precision-weighted mean mₙ. Integrating μ out of the next-month likelihood adds vₙ to σ². Thus the 95% credible interval for mean cash is [16,348, 33,652], while the 95% predictive interval for next-month cash is [7,091, 42,909] KES. Both centre on KES25,000. The latter is wider because an individual month can differ from the underlying mean.

For a proposed instalment a, the model supplies P(Cₙ₊₁ < a | C). It is 6.27% for KES11,000 and 86.31% for KES35,000. The declared policy additionally caps the payment at 50% of posterior mean cash, KES12,500, and requires a complete source window, resolved material cash classes and a passing dated cash calendar. The 10% predictive-shortfall tolerance and 50% cap are policy assumptions in this example. They can be varied in the parameter file. The amount is considered by simulated maker and checker records after these calculations.

Part 2b §11, “Decision quantities and the policy boundary”, supplies the broader architecture. The five-article series develops posterior action comparisons and limit-review boundaries in Article 2, “Bayesian decision boundaries turn predictions into limit-review evidence”, and distinguishes uncertainties in Article 5, “Show the reviewer three kinds of uncertainty”. A hierarchical Student-t coefficient model, half-t group-scale priors, Pólya–Gamma computation and a causal intervention model have distinct roles in that larger programme. This cash-case Normal model is the fully implemented computation shown here; the references identify where the broader methods are developed. [1], [4]

## Facility terms and the cash calendar

The illustrated revised facility advances KES30,000 on day 1 and requires KES11,000 on days 30,60 and90: KES10,000 principal and KES1,000 fee per instalment. Its total fee is KES3,000. This is a teaching contract, with no representation of Moniepoint pricing or terms. Opening merchant cash is KES50,000. Five receipts of KES82,000 arrive on days 6,12,18,24 and30 of a normal month. Supplier payments of KES54,000 fall on days 1,7,13,19 and25. Operating payments fall on days 10,20 and30; household needs fall on day 28 and existing debt on day 30. The normal calendar reaches a minimum KES26,000, above the KES20,000 collection reserve.

The loan finances part of an existing working-capital cash requirement. Its disbursement is financing cash and creates a matching principal liability. The model assumes the same contractual trading-receipt path across financing alternatives; it measures cash timing and loan affordability under that path. Repeat applications are prescribed fixtures, permitted only after full previous settlement, a seven-day cooling-off period, at least thirty observed days with residual cash of KES22,000 or more, settled operating bills and sufficient cash. Each has its own simulated proposal and checker approval. A live repeat-facility assessment would additionally document a renewed business purpose, funding gap and customer value before using these capacity gates.

The case is a contracted cash-calendar model. Supplier and operating obligations continue to accrue in stress, and unpaid amounts remain liabilities. This convention measures the funding and workout requirement under the stated plan; an inventory-and-trading extension would specify supplier credit limits, curtailed purchases, lost sales and changes to operations when that plan becomes infeasible. Household shortfalls are recorded separately. Trading obligations and existing debts have priority over collection on the new facility; new-loan payment uses cash above the declared reserve. Original dues, approved modified dues, principal allocations, fees, arrears and receipts remain separately visible.

Source defects have different consequences from economic deterioration. Deduplication and paired transfers preserve the capacity calculation. Missing coverage and unresolved classifications lead to evidence requests and no funded facility in the fixture. Those unfunded rows have no observed repayment label. The post-disbursement feed-gap case retains the baseline economic outcome but routes the reported fall in receipts to source repair. This is the practical connection to Article 3, “Define the potential outcome under a complete contract” and “What a hierarchical Bayesian model contributes”: the observation process and the outcome process require distinct records. [4]

The settlement-delay case shifts the second month's receipts by fifteen days. The first facility reaches eight days past due and ultimately repays. A reviewed extension, agreed on day 45 using evidence available then, moves the remaining dues from days 60 and90 to days 75 and105. Contractual DPD becomes zero, while collected cash and household experience still require separate assessment. Both delayed scenarios record a KES20,000 household shortfall. Their higher terminal cash includes that foregone household payment. It therefore needs to be read beside the shortfall, rather than treated as an improvement in customer welfare.

Part 1 §1, “When a profitable month still misses Tuesday's instalment”, motivates this dated view; Part 3 §2, “The intervention contract”, and §4, “The Credit Policy and Compliance Gate”, motivate preserving the agreed change and its authority. The reschedule here is a prescribed scenario response. Article 1's offer experiments and Article 2's sequential collection design describe how an empirical programme could estimate whether such a response improves outcomes, using defined allocation, receipt, follow-up and comparison rules. A scenario difference quantifies the consequences of the stipulated contract and cash assumptions; measured treatment effects require that additional design. [1], [4], [5]

## Lender cash and the cost of time

For every loan, repayments clear the oldest due instalment first, with principal then fee within that instalment. Partial payments therefore preserve the age of each outstanding amount. Default is an explicit thirty-day-past-due teaching rule; it transfers outstanding principal to workout. A recovery review takes place thirty days later, subject to merchant cash, settled operating obligations and the collection reserve. The severe sales scenario yields KES20,000 outstanding default principal and zero available recovery at that review. Funding continues on unrecovered principal through day 300, so a default entry does not make financing cost disappear.

The lender's funding rate is 12% per annum on a 365-day basis. Each origination costs KES500, servicing costs KES2 per performing loan-day and workout costs KES400. Contribution is measured before central overhead, tax and a capital hurdle:

Π = collected fees − funding cost − origination and servicing costs − workout cost − principal written off + cash recoveries.

The balance control is:

Originations = principal repaid + gross principal written off + terminal performing principal.

The cash control is:

Π = cumulative lender net cash + terminal performing principal.

Recovery is included in net cash and contribution; unpaid default principal is separately reported as a workout exposure. The output also records accrued unpaid fees. These are realised contribution and ledger identities under the declared recognition conventions. Part 5 §6, “Collection timing becomes a treasury variable”, §7, “ALM and matched-maturity FTP translate cash timing into product economics”, and Article 4's worked FTP section explain the next step toward institution-specific transfer pricing and capital costs. [1], [4]

The baseline yields KES5,184.66. The settlement-delay case yields KES5,155.07; the reviewed extension yields KES5,056.03. Persistent sales loss produces KES-22,151.23, together with unpaid trading obligations, household shortfalls and residual default principal. These accompanying balances explain why collection and sustainability are assessed together.

## The evidence and decision contract

Each case has a correlation ID. Each evidence snapshot, proposal, checker approval, disbursement receipt, monitoring route, revised schedule, closure and workout event has an event ID. Records include time, actor role, evidence digest, model version, policy version, details and previous-event hash. The human actors are simulated fixtures that demonstrate how a real authorised workflow would record decisions. The calculation service and policy route remain separate from that authority. The hash chain detects a changed record; the retained sources and permission/approval records establish its substantive meaning.

Part 4 §3, “One event fabric, several accountable records”, §6, “Promote a complete artefact and preserve a safe return path”, and §7, “Experiment and enterprise records must join at the decision”, directly inform this implementation. Article 5 develops the controlled deployment workflow and meaningful review. BCBS 239 supports the broader discipline of accuracy, completeness, timeliness and reconciliation in risk data. A production adapter would also implement revocation, access enforcement, idempotent execution, approved exceptions and customer correction routes at the bank boundary. [1], [4], [6]

Personal-data handling follows the applicable purpose, minimisation, lawful-processing and rights requirements. The Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 includes principles in section 25, DPIA requirements in section 31 and automated-decision provisions in section 35. The General Regulations 2021 add automated-decision safeguards in regulation 22. This synthetic case contains no customer identities; a bank implementation should bind each source and tool to its approved use and preserve meaningful information and reconsideration routes. The article carries the fuller sector applicability discussion. [7], [8]

## Deterministic measures and governed judgment

The deterministic baseline belongs inside this workflow. Provenance-adjusted trailing receipts, reconciled balances, known obligations, a dated payment calendar, cash reserves and arrears provide replayable measures against which a proposed recommendation can be tested. A rolling average changes as observations arrive; its calculation rule can remain fixed and versioned. The current case implements a thirty-day trailing residual gate for repeat review. Seven-day and ninety-day receipt measures are useful proposed extensions, each requiring a cutoff, observation window, minimum coverage, units, seasonality treatment and documented handling of missing data.

Those controls have distinct roles. A balance identity or duplicate check validates the evidence; a hard eligibility gate limits the available actions; an approved threshold implements credit policy; a rolling average describes recent activity; a statistical model quantifies uncertainty; and a causal study estimates the consequence of changing an action. Agents can investigate a missing channel, propose a cash classification or prepare an exception packet around this foundation. Versioned rules and human mandates govern the resulting action, and an override retains its reason, approver and expiry. The EBA's loan-origination and monitoring guidance is a useful international comparator for lifecycle governance and early-warning indicators, while NIST's AI Risk Management Framework gives a complementary structure for human–AI roles and accountability. Kenya's legal requirements continue to govern the local application. [9], [10]

## Verification and reproducibility

Run from the repository root using Python 3.10 or later:

```powershell
python -B research_lab/moniepoint_cashflow_case/run_case.py
python -B -m unittest discover -s research_lab/moniepoint_cashflow_case -p test_model.py -v
python -B research_lab/moniepoint_cashflow_case/build_charts.py
```

The numerical engine uses the standard library; the chart builder uses Pillow and Arial. Supply an alternative parameter JSON with `--inputs` and a separate destination with `--output` to retain the published run. The tests cover exact source totals, internal transfers, duplicate conflicts, future-information exclusion, classification vintages, predictive/credible intervals, affordability and calendar gates, maker–checker ordering, audit hashes, FIFO repayments, repeat eligibility, funding after default, cash, debt, fees, household shortfalls and deterministic reproduction.

`output/moniepoint_cashflow_case_2026-10-18` contains scenario summaries, decision snapshots, source records, separate synthetic truth, classified records, source coverage, account links, daily cash, instalment allocations, loan records, monitoring routes and event logs. `verification.json` records source/parameter hashes and the reconciliation results. Exactly 36 tests pass in the recorded build. The chart PNGs are exported separately for uploading with the LinkedIn article.
