**Sukrit Ghildiyal** — Sukrit’s comment on **Geofrey Osiemo’s** post about Support Vector Machines and IFRS 9 prompted the second LinkedIn article. It sharpened the question of whether cash moving through an account is genuinely generated by the business or is a related-party transfer or other temporary inflow. That question became the entity- and transaction-level cash-provenance framing carried into this paper. [Geofrey’s original post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/geofrey-osiemo-706b0a8b_support-vector-machines-svm-meets-ifrs-activity-7505350598775521280-1DSs) began the discussion.

**CPA James K. and Felix Sifuna** — Their discussion of NPL ratios as lagging indicators, together with my response, helped shape the first LinkedIn article, *Underwrite for Collection*, and sharpened Part 1’s treatment of industry metrics. It led me to ask what aggregate NPL ratios reveal about lending and recovery, and what they can obscure when the denominator or loan book changes. The chapter now distinguishes dates, populations and denominators, and places aggregate ratios alongside the lifecycle cash, cohort, cure and recovery measures the research question requires.

**Beata Lubinska, Ph.D.** — Beata’s posts and course material helped shape the paper’s practical questions on ALM, IRRBB, non-maturing deposits, FTP and ALCO: [ALM and balance-sheet management](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/beata-lubinska_i-am-delighted-to-be-joining-forces-with-activity-7466119487281557504-9BD-) and [FTP in product pricing and balance-sheet decisions](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/beata-lubinska_excited-to-kick-off-the-next-ftp-course-starting-activity-7416178996964499456-VPNj).
