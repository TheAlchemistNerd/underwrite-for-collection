# Part 5 Brief

## Working title

**KESONIA Pricing, Expected Credit Loss, Capital and Funding**

## Scale

- Full-source target: 6,186 words.
- Editorial-prose target: 5,214 words.

## Purpose

Part 5 translates lifecycle evidence into customer price, accounting, capital, liquidity, funding and risk-transfer consequences without conflating these measures.

## Core sections

1. KESONIA benchmark conventions and customer-pricing architecture.
2. `K_RBCP` decomposition and governance.
3. Physical default risk, expected loss and market-consistent valuation.
4. IFRS 9 staging, scenario weighting, modification, cure, recovery and ECL.
5. Regulatory capital and internal economic capital.
6. Collection cash flows, behavioural maturity and liquidity.
7. Bank ALM and funds-transfer pricing.
8. Risk-adjusted product and intervention economics.
9. Guarantees, credit insurance, funded and synthetic risk transfer.
10. Optional ring-fenced receivables financing and ecosystem capital.

## Required reconciliations

- reference benchmark versus customer premium;
- expected loss versus accounting ECL;
- accounting allowance versus regulatory and economic capital;
- operating cost versus collection cost;
- liquidity cost versus credit spread;
- bank FTP versus investor hurdle;
- gross versus net loss after guarantee or insurance;
- model improvement versus approved accounting or capital recognition.

## Quantitative continuity

The paper will connect lifecycle state probabilities to contractual and behavioural cash flows. Collection frequency, recovery timing, modification and re-default enter discounted cash-flow, ECL, liquidity-gap and capital calculations through explicit assumptions. Lower predicted risk creates no automatic balance-sheet benefit.

## Acceptance tests

- KESONIA is treated as time varying and date-specific.
- Customer pricing follows the applicable CBK structure and does not use experimental treatment effects as `K_RBCP`.
- All quantities have compatible units, horizons and denominators.
- Collection cadence is not assumed to be liquidity until settlement and stress behaviour are tested.
- Risk-transfer price includes attachment, exhaustion, counterparty, basis and recovery mechanics.
