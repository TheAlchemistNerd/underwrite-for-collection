# Part 1 Brief

## Working title

**Underwrite for Collection: The MSME Credit Lifecycle and Collection Cascades**

## Scale

- Full-source target: 4,883 words.
- Editorial-prose target: 4,434 words.

## Purpose

Part 1 establishes the human, microeconomic, commercial and institutional reason for the series. It reframes collectability as an upstream design objective rather than a late operational response to delinquency.

## Governing question

How can a lender design MSME credit so that product terms, information, servicing and intervention support sustainable collection after disbursement?

## Narrative route

1. Open with an MSME whose sales, supplier obligations, receivables, inventory, payroll, taxes and household withdrawals follow different clocks.
2. Explain why static origination evidence can miss the timing structure of repayment capacity.
3. Separate liquidity, solvency, willingness, operational disruption and recoverability.
4. Define collection, cure, durable cure and net recovery.
5. Show how product design can create or reduce a default cascade.
6. Introduce continuous lifecycle evidence and supportive intervention.
7. Map the wider ecosystem and institutional responsibilities.
8. Present the research programme and the role of Parts 2a–6.

## Required diagrams

- MSME operating cash-flow mismatch and credit cascade.
- Lifecycle credit loop from product design to governed learning.
- Ecosystem map covering the MSME, bank, bureau, payments, KBA, regulators, DFI/guarantee providers, insurers where relevant, researchers and service partners.

## Evidence priorities

- Current KBA and CBK MSME lending and asset-quality material.
- Kenyan MSME economic and sector context from authoritative sources.
- Primary Kenyan regulatory sources for institution and product descriptions.
- Literature on relationship banking, cash-flow lending, early warning and collection outcomes.

## Boundaries

Part 1 will not introduce the full HLR, causal estimators, KESONIA equations or enterprise stack. It will motivate those components and point to their dedicated parts.

## Acceptance tests

- A reader can explain the difference between origination quality and lifecycle collectability.
- Aggregate lending flows and NPL stocks are compared only with their correct scope and denominator.
- The ecosystem is collaborative without implying unconfirmed institutional commitments.
- The opening remains relatable while every technical promise is later traceable.
