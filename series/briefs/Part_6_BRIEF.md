# Part 6 Brief

## Working title

**Randomised Pilots, Quasi-Experiments and Ecosystem Execution**

## Scale

- Full-source target: 5,155 words.
- Editorial-prose target: 4,592 words.

## Purpose

Part 6 turns the research architecture into a bank-ready and ecosystem-supported implementation programme.

## Governing question

What sequence of evidence, governance and partnership is required to move from a strong concept to a safe, measurable and scalable MSME credit intervention?

## Programme stages

1. Define the problem with banks, MSMEs and ecosystem stakeholders.
2. Establish public-data and synthetic baselines.
3. Agree data roles, lawful bases, protocol ownership and publication rights.
4. Build within a bank-controlled research environment.
5. Reconstruct point-in-time histories and perform retrospective validation.
6. Run prospective shadow scoring.
7. Conduct a restricted randomised or quasi-experimental pilot.
8. Complete independent model, conduct, privacy and accounting review.
9. Scale by product, institution or sector under monitored gates.
10. Publish disclosure-controlled results and feed evidence into product and policy design.

## Pilot families

- evidence-use pilot;
- repayment-structure pilot;
- supportive intervention pilot;
- restructuring and durable-cure pilot;
- optional guarantee or risk-sharing pilot.

These families will not be collapsed into one experiment. Separate assignment and outcome structures preserve interpretation.

## Ecosystem roles

The proposed coalition may include participating banks, KBA, MSME representative bodies, relevant regulators, an independent research and validation team, payments or data partners, credit bureaus, DFIs or guarantee providers, and technology vendors. Participation will be described as proposed until confirmed.

## Required experimental detail

- target population and unit;
- cluster or individual assignment;
- stratification and power;
- minimum detectable effect;
- spillovers and contamination;
- delayed outcomes and censoring;
- noncompliance and attrition;
- primary and secondary estimands;
- pre-registration and statistical-analysis plan;
- interim review and stop rules;
- complaints, hardship and fairness monitoring;
- reproducibility and controlled disclosure.

## Acceptance tests

- The roadmap is achievable without assuming immediate access to anonymised customer-level data.
- Randomised and quasi-experimental routes are selected by feasibility and identification, not fashion.
- Adaptive learning appears only after conventional evidence and safeguards.
- Regulatory engagement is described accurately and without invented timelines.
- Each phase has entry criteria, exit evidence, owner, artifact and rollback route.
