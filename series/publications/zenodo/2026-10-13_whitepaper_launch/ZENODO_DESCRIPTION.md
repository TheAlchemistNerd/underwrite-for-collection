Underwrite for Collection develops a proposed architecture for sustainable micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) credit across the facility lifecycle. Its central research question asks which product structures, evidence sources and proportionate interventions improve sustainable collection for defined Kenyan MSME populations, at acceptable risk, cost and customer impact.

The completed research edition brings together six manuscripts across five parts. Part 1 connects the enterprise’s operating cycle, loan design and collection outcomes. Part 2a develops point-in-time evidence, entity and transaction-level cash provenance, and lifecycle features. Part 2b presents hierarchical Bayesian underwriting, uncertainty quantification, causal identification, survival and multi-state modelling, and portfolio dependence. Part 3 connects evidence to fair intervention through defined eligibility, human review, decision authority, customer agreement and follow-up. Part 4 sets out a bank-controlled research and enterprise integration architecture. Part 5 connects lifecycle cash flows to KESONIA-linked pricing, expected credit loss, capital, liquidity, asset and liability management, funds transfer pricing and risk transfer.

Two reproducible worked cases accompany the manuscripts. The first follows a hypothetical MSME order through operating cash, financing, repayment timing, lender earnings, discounted recovery, guarantee settlement and an optional ring-fenced receivables structure. The second reconstructs operating cash from transaction records and follows evidence review, an authorised loan amendment, contract booking, settlement and protection re-verification. Declared assumptions, calculation code, input records and automated tests make the cases reproducible through the linked research lab.

The paper distinguishes financing an operating business from financing ring-fenced receivables and explains how the two connect through repayment and collection flows. Its Kenyan governance framework incorporates the Data Protection Act 2019, the Data Protection General Regulations 2021 and relevant institutional responsibilities.

Developed from a LinkedIn article series and practical questions about MSME lending, the whitepaper provides a research foundation for credit practitioners, actuaries, researchers and institutional teams. Its methods and hypothetical cases support empirical calibration, prospective evaluation and implementation planning.
