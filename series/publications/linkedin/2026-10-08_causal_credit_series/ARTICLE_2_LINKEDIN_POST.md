From a One-Step Bandit to Sequential Credit Control | Article 2 of 5

A collection decision affects more than today's payment. A revised schedule, supportive contact or caseworker referral can change the borrower's next decision, the lender's information and the prospects of sustained repayment.

Building on Article 1's credit-offer experiments, this article follows the account after disbursement. It develops the progression from contextual bandits to sequential control around three research questions:

1. Among legally and operationally eligible Kenyan MSME credit accounts, what is the causal effect of a support-oriented collection action on verified net cash recovered and sustained repayment, relative to standard servicing?

2. Under what data, overlap, selection and governance assumptions can contextual-bandit or sequential off-policy evaluation estimate the value of alternative collection policies, and how sensitive are the estimates to reject inference and unobserved caseworker information?

3. When do repeated actions, borrower-state transitions and partially observed cash-flow conditions justify moving from randomized pilots and contextual bandits to model-predictive control, constrained MDPs or POMDPs, while preserving affordability, privacy, fairness and meaningful human review?

Worked illustrations connect repayment states, partial observation and policy evaluation. A separate diagram explains how relationship graphs, LinUCB, logistic Thompson sampling, Monte Carlo tree search and graph explanations contribute to different decisions.

I welcome experiences from collections, servicing and credit-model teams.
