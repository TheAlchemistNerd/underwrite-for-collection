Testing Credit Offers for Collectability | Article 1 of 5

The design of a credit offer shapes the repayment journey that follows. For an MSME, the amount, price and payment schedule need to connect to how the business earns and uses cash.

This first article extends Underwrite for Collection into randomized credit-offer experiments and contextual bandits. It connects offer assignment, take-up, delayed repayment evidence and customer outcomes through three research questions:

1. Among eligible Kenyan MSME applicants, what is the effect of a cash-flow-aligned credit offer on take-up, durable repayment, net realized cash and borrower outcomes compared with a defined standard offer?

2. When does response-adaptive contextual allocation improve offer learning enough to justify its loss of fixed-allocation simplicity, given delayed outcomes, changing cohorts and limited overlap?

3. What experimental, data-protection, fairness and human-review safeguards are needed before contextual bandits can inform credit offers or later collection actions in the Underwrite for Collection lifecycle?

The article includes a worked offer example, delayed-outcome and competing-risk formulations, an experiment flowchart and numbered references. The design connects learning to affordability, data protection and accountable review.

I welcome perspectives from colleagues designing MSME products and credit experiments.
