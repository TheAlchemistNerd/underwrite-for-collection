Can Reinforcement Learning Help Run a Bank's Balance Sheet? | Article 4 of 5

Credit offers, collection actions and repayment evidence also affect the institution financing the loans. This fourth article follows that connection into Treasury and asset-liability management.

It brings KESONIA, internal funds transfer pricing, behavioural cash flows and ALCO decisions into a constrained stochastic-control framework. Three research questions structure the discussion:

1. How should a Kenyan bank connect KESONIA-based customer pricing, internal FTP, curve construction and behavioural deposit assumptions so that product profitability reconciles with Treasury funding costs and ALCO risk measures over the facility lifecycle?

2. Under what data, simulation, constraint and governance conditions can stochastic control or reinforcement learning improve Treasury decisions relative to transparent baselines while preserving NII, EVE, liquidity, capital, collateral and operational limits?

3. Which data-protection and model-governance controls are required when Treasury analytics use identifiable deposit, borrower, guarantor or employee data, and when does a workflow move from institutional optimization to a potentially significant automated decision about an individual?

The article connects funding and hedge decisions to net interest income, economic value, liquidity, capital and collateral. Worked illustrations and diagrams show how transparent baselines, simulation and human approval fit together.

Beata Lubinska, Ph.D.’s writing on Treasury and ALM, including her discussion of the deep-learning ALM prototype developed by Mariusz Podsiadlo, Ph.D., CFA, helped inspire this line of inquiry. Both are acknowledged in the Underwrite for Collection whitepaper.

I welcome perspectives from Treasury, ALM, Risk and Finance colleagues.
