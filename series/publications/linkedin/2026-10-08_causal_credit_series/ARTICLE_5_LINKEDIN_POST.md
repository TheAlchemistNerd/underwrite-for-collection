Bringing the Methods into the Bank | Article 5 of 5

How do credit experiments, sequential collections, reject inference and Treasury models become an accountable banking workflow?

This final article connects the first four through a bank-controlled environment: governed evidence, approved analytical tools, uncertainty-aware human review, authorised execution and reconciled outcomes. It extends Part 4 of the Underwrite for Collection whitepaper around three research questions:

1. How can a bank-controlled agentic workflow connect offer experiments, sequential collections, selection diagnostics and Treasury analysis while preserving purpose-bound data access, separate decision authority and reconciled execution?

2. How should credible intervals, exceedance probabilities and predictive cash distributions be presented to human reviewers so that uncertainty changes limit, servicing and funding decisions constructively?

3. What evidence and operating tests should govern progression from a research sandbox to shadow use and bounded deployment under Kenya’s Data Protection Act and General Regulations?

A hypothetical MSME case shows why a 4% average default estimate, its credible interval and the probability of exceeding a risk threshold can lead to different review choices. The article also follows supportive contact through approval, delivery, payment and settlement, while keeping the lender’s credit decision and Treasury’s funding decision accountable to their respective owners.

The accompanying workflow diagram brings branch access, restricted AI tools, meaningful human review, customer correction and validated learning into one operating design.
