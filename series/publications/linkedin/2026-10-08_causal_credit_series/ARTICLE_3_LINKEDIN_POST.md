Who Gets a Repayment Label? | Article 3 of 5

The first two articles examine how to learn from credit offers and collection decisions. This third article asks whose outcomes enter the evidence in the first place.

Historical repayment records reflect approval, offer acceptance, drawdown and servicing decisions. Understanding that selection is central to reject inference and to assessing how far a model's conclusions travel. Three research questions guide the article:

1. In which Kenyan MSME segments and contract types do historical funded-loan records provide enough overlap to estimate repayment risk, and how do conclusions change under plausible unobserved-selection assumptions?

2. What randomized offer or repayment-schedule design can generate useful evidence near an approval boundary while preserving affordability, data-protection safeguards, human review and ordinary borrower rights?

3. How should borrower cash provenance, ring-fenced receivables, collective guarantees and realized collection outcomes enter a validated risk framework without treating imputed reject labels as observed repayment or conflating ECL with capital?

The discussion connects cash provenance, receivables and collective guarantees to selective labels. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac performance-data documentation provides a practical reference for cohort definitions and outcome histories.

I welcome perspectives on approval boundaries, model validation and better evidence collection.
