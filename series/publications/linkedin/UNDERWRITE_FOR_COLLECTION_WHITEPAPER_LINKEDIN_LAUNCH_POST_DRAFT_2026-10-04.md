## Post
**Underwrite for Collection: a completed research whitepaper**

This work began with a practical question: how can lenders support sustainable MSME repayment across a facility’s life?

What started as LinkedIn articles is now a completed research edition of six manuscripts across five parts, with two reproducible cases. It follows MSME credit from product design and cash-flow evidence through underwriting, fair servicing, monitoring, collection, recovery, expected credit loss, capital and funding.

The cases connect enterprise finance to ring-fenced receivables through dated cash. In Part 1, a KES 200,000 customer advance and KES 800,000 completion receipt fund an order while the KES 500,000 loan falls due on day 60. If the final receipt arrives on day 90, the forecast reaches minus KES 395,000; preserving the KES 100,000 operating floor requires KES 495,000 of additional funding. That tests the business cash cycle against its debt schedule. Part 5 follows the bank’s loan earnings, funding cost, expected loss and recovery. Its optional ring-fenced case compares the bank retaining loans through resolution with a vehicle buying ten KES 500,000 claims on borrower loan repayments. The customer invoices remain in each enterprise’s cash flow; after transfer, borrower repayments enter vehicle accounts and the waterfall allocates cash to expenses, notes, reserves and residual equity. Investor equity cash is KES 830,000 in the base case, KES 814,777.78 under timing stress and KES 451,844.44 under default stress.

Exploring the IFoA introduction to banking module gave me an academic starting point and an actuarial lens for these practical lending questions.

I’m grateful to Judy Chege, whose writing informed the credit-lifecycle framing; Sukrit Ghildiyal, whose comment on Geofrey Osiemo’s SVM/IFRS 9 post prompted the second article and cash-provenance analysis; and CPA James K. and Felix Sifuna, whose exchange on lagging NPL ratios and my reply helped shape the first article and Part 1. The chapter now distinguishes dates, populations and denominators, and places aggregate ratios alongside the lifecycle cash, cohort, cure and recovery measures the research question requires. Beata Lubinska and Mariusz Podsiadlo’s Treasury and ALM discussions also shaped the paper.
