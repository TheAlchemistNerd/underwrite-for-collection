# Experimental and Causal Design Standard

## Purpose

This standard governs claims about whether a product, message, repayment structure, servicing action or recovery treatment changes an MSME outcome. Predictive accuracy alone is not causal evidence.

## Minimum design record

Every study must state:

- decision and business question;
- target population and unit of analysis;
- treatment and comparator;
- assignment mechanism;
- primary and secondary estimands;
- outcome definitions and observation horizons;
- eligibility, exclusions and consent or other lawful-basis analysis;
- power or precision basis;
- stratification, clustering and spillover risks;
- treatment adherence, crossover and missingness;
- estimation method and uncertainty;
- multiplicity and subgroup policy;
- interim review and stop rules;
- conduct, fairness, complaints and vulnerability safeguards;
- data-retention, access and disclosure controls;
- pre-registration and independent review status.

## Experimental sequence

1. Retrospective feasibility and outcome-definition study.
2. Synthetic or replay evaluation of the operational pipeline.
3. Prospective shadow scoring with no customer effect.
4. Conventional randomised pilot where lawful and ethical.
5. Quasi-experimental study where assignment cannot be randomised but identification is credible.
6. Adaptive allocation only after stable outcomes, logging and safeguards have been demonstrated.

## Recommended outcomes

Repayment alone is insufficient. Depending on the intervention, the outcome family should include:

- missed payment and days past due;
- transition to material arrears;
- cure and durable cure;
- re-default;
- modification and restructuring;
- prepayment;
- default and charge-off;
- gross and net recovery;
- workout, communication and operational cost;
- complaint and dispute incidence;
- customer contact burden;
- business continuity or survival proxies;
- ECL, capital and funding consequences;
- distributional outcomes across protected or vulnerable groups where lawful and appropriate.

## Randomised designs

Candidate designs include individual randomisation, cluster randomisation, stepped-wedge rollout, encouragement design and factorial experiments. The design must account for operational spillovers, officer behaviour, branch effects, sector shocks and delayed credit outcomes.

Randomisation does not legitimise a treatment that is unlawful, unaffordable, deceptive or inconsistent with approved hardship and vulnerability policy. High-risk or urgent cases may require exclusion and mandatory human handling.

## Quasi-experimental designs

Candidate designs include:

- regression discontinuity around a genuine assignment threshold;
- difference-in-differences with pre-trend and concurrent-shock analysis;
- interrupted time series with sufficient pre- and post-observations;
- synthetic control for aggregate institutional or regional interventions;
- instrumental variables with defensible relevance and exclusion restrictions;
- target-trial emulation with explicit time zero, treatment strategies and censoring rules.

The design label is not evidence by itself. Each study must document diagnostics, falsification tests and the plausible direction of residual bias.

## Selective labels and rejected applicants

Credit outcomes are observed mainly for applicants who receive facilities. The programme will not treat rejected applicants as known non-performers. Where learning about the approval margin is necessary, options include limited controlled exploration within policy bounds, threshold-based designs, external outcome linkage where lawful, partial-identification analysis and sensitivity bounds.

## Adaptive policies

Contextual bandits or other adaptive allocation methods are later-stage challengers. Their use requires:

- stable and sufficiently prompt reward definitions;
- logged action propensities;
- positivity floors and protected control allocation;
- delayed-outcome and censoring treatment;
- off-policy evaluation;
- non-stationarity monitoring;
- conduct and fairness constraints;
- human override and rollback;
- independent governance approval.

## Interpretation rule

A statistically credible average effect does not establish affordability, fairness, profitability, accounting treatment or capital relief. Each downstream claim requires its own analysis.
