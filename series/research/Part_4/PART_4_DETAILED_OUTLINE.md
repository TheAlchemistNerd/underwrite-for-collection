# Part 4 Detailed Outline

## Working title

**Bank-Controlled Research Sandboxes and Enterprise Credit Architecture**

## Governing question

How can a bank bring point-in-time lifecycle evidence, an approved Bayesian model, governed interventions and prospective research into its operating environment while customer data, lending authority and the system of record remain under institutional control?

## Narrative and analytical sequence

1. **Bring the method to the bank.** Begin with a relationship manager who can see a viable enterprise and a temporary cash-flow mismatch, while the research team sees only a development schema. Establish why useful research does not require unrestricted customer-data export.
2. **Define the bank-controlled boundary.** Separate production systems, the governed evidence plane, research workspaces, the approved scoring service, the Credit Policy and Compliance Gate and downstream systems. State that the bank retains lending authority and accountability.
3. **Build one replayable event fabric.** Carry the event-time, knowledge-time, correction-time and decision-cutoff concepts from Part 2a into source ownership, canonical identifiers, double-entry reconciliation, data-quality controls and immutable decision packets.
4. **Move code to data.** Define purpose-bound workspaces, pseudonymous keys, separately controlled re-identification, role and attribute-based access, time-limited credentials, output disclosure control and a living DPIA.
5. **Progress from synthetic development to institutional evidence.** Use public and synthetic data for schema, software, estimand and failure testing; then move through secure retrospective reconstruction, shadow scoring and a separately governed live pilot.
6. **Use privacy-enhancing technology proportionately.** Establish the secure single-bank workspace as the default. Position federated learning, secure aggregation, differential privacy and trusted execution as optional responses to specific multi-institution, output or infrastructure threats.
7. **Promote an approved artefact, not an active training loop.** Define the signed model bundle, registry states, independent validation, release gates, deterministic scoring, fallback, rollback and emergency controls.
8. **Make experiment evidence part of the enterprise record.** Store eligibility, assignment, propensity, delivery, crossover, contamination, exposure, outcome maturity and conduct events so intention-to-treat and per-protocol estimands can be reconstructed.
9. **Integrate through explicit system contracts.** Map the scoring and policy services to origination, core banking, payments, servicing, collections, finance, ECL, capital and treasury without making a general ledger or vendor interface the calculation owner for unsupported logic.
10. **Govern the ecosystem as an operating service.** Define first, second and third-line responsibilities, service levels, third-party due diligence, resilience, disclosure review and publication controls.
11. **Conclusion.** Present the sandbox as an institutional learning system that preserves customer rights and bank accountability while turning lifecycle underwriting into reproducible evidence.

## Mathematical and computational content

- Reproducible decision packet and hash-linked artefact identity.
- Role, purpose, environment and time intersection for data access.
- Disclosure-release rule based on minimum cell size, sensitivity and privacy budget where applicable.
- Federated objective with institution weights, accompanied by heterogeneity and poisoning qualifications.
- Deterministic equivalence test between validation and production scoring.
- Experiment record sufficient for intention-to-treat, treatment-received and off-policy estimands.
- Source-to-subledger and subledger-to-control-total reconciliation identities.
