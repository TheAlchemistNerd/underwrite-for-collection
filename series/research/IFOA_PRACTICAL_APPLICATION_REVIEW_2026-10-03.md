## U1 — Credit and workout decision under incomplete evidence

**Owner roles:** credit officer, collections lead and independent reviewer. **Chapter homes:** Parts 1, 3 and 5.

Take the existing order/cash case through a delayed receipt and fresh information about other debt, legal protection and actual end-use. Compare continued terms, a defined amendment and a feasible recovery/workout action. Separate lending to the operating business from financing a specific assigned cash stream: define the obligor, claim, recourse, leakage, account control and competing creditors for each.

**Deliverables:** frozen credit file, dated cash forecasts, alternatives including current policy, decision memo and customer explanation.

**Completion evidence:** the chosen action fits borrower cash and lender authority; information classification cannot create money; legal/protection assumptions have evidence dates and owners. Report borrower viability, net recovery, time to cash and customer burden. Show when a different assumption changes the recommendation.

## U2 — Incremental intervention value

**Owner roles:** research/validation and collections operations. **Chapter homes:** Parts 2b and 3.

Define an eligible population, treatment, comparison policy, decision time and outcome window. Outcomes should include sustainable cure, repeat delinquency, net cash after servicing costs, business survival and customer harm. Distinguish accelerating an inevitable receipt from increasing ultimate recovery.

**Deliverables:** experiment or observational identification protocol, assignment/selection audit, attrition and censoring treatment, pre-specified decision rule and sensitivity analysis.

**Completion evidence:** baseline and follow-up windows are fixed before analysis; the comparison deals explicitly with confounding and competing exits; uncertainty affects the action. Recovery/write-off, prepayment and intermediate delinquency remain distinct from cure. If randomisation is feasible, evaluate consent, operational capacity and spillovers; otherwise state the assumptions supporting the chosen causal design. A measured association alone cannot close the causal requirement.

## U3 — Continuous protection and evidence monitoring

**Owner roles:** servicing, legal/protection specialist and operations. **Chapter homes:** Parts 2a, 3 and 4.

Extend the evidence case with lien priority changes, tax status, collateral deterioration, insurer-cover changes and related-entity events. Separate borrower default signals from weakening recovery protection. Every alert needs a response owner, evidence refresh interval and escalation path.

**Deliverables:** longitudinal event log, source freshness report, permission model, uncertainty/missingness policy and exception-resolution history.

**Completion evidence:** only information available at the decision time is used; a disputed alert does not silently become a fact; a protection change reaches the loss/recovery scenario even when payments remain current. Simulate a failed source feed and an overdue review. Independent replay must recover the decision-time record and any later correction.

## U4 — Lifecycle profitability and professional handover

**Owner roles:** Finance, credit policy and independent model reviewer. **Chapter homes:** Part 5 and public appendices.

Use actual or clearly labelled hypothetical servicing and workout costs to bridge original price, contractual interest, default/forbearance, extended funding, capital time and realised collections. Add scenario probabilities only with a stated estimation or judgement basis. Keep economic pricing, IFRS 9 measurement and regulatory capital separately defined.

**Deliverables:** cash-to-earnings bridge, alternative-action valuation, model audit trail and actual-versus-expected report. Reuse the existing Decimal ledgers and convention definitions; consume Treasury rates through RegTech's dated interface.

**Completion evidence:** no loss or funding cost is deducted twice; a reviewer can reproduce the result and locate every material assumption; cash, accounting income and owner distributions remain distinct. A senior reader can identify the decision and its uncertainty without reading code.
