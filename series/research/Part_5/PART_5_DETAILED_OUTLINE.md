# Part 5 Detailed Outline

## Working title

**KESONIA Pricing, Expected Credit Loss, Capital and Funding**

## Intended outcome

Part 5 converts the lifecycle evidence developed in Parts 1 to 4 into financial consequences that can be owned by credit, finance, treasury, capital, collections and risk-transfer functions. It retains a human MSME narrative while giving every quantity a defined purpose, horizon, probability basis, denominator, discount rule and institutional owner.

The manuscript will target 6,186 full-source words and at least 5,214 words of editorial prose, excluding references. It will use three large portrait diagrams with straight, orthogonal Mermaid connectors and legible node text consistent with Parts 1 to 4.

## Narrative spine

The opening case follows an MSME whose approved facility is quoted over KESONIA. A benchmark reset changes contractual interest, but the enterprise's repayment capacity depends on trading cash flow, payment timing and the intervention pathway. The same account can therefore generate different quantities for customer pricing, expected loss, accounting impairment, economic capital, liquidity, FTP and risk transfer. Part 5 follows the cash from scheduled obligation to realised collection and shows why those quantities must remain connected but distinct.

## Section map and approximate allocation

### Abstract, approximately 180 words

State the principal contribution: one governed cash-flow distribution can support several financial decisions when each decision applies its own measure and approval framework.

### 1. Price begins with a benchmark, approximately 650 words

- Introduce KESONIA through the MSME obligation rather than as a rate-engine manual.
- Define KESONIA, the compounded index, business-day publication and compounded-in-arrears use.
- State the revised customer-pricing identity.
- Explain contract conventions: source, observation window, day count, compounding, lookback or payment delay, floor, cap, reset, fallback and correction.
- Keep a dated market observation illustrative and outside the contract definition.

### 2. `K_RBCP` is a governed commercial premium, approximately 550 words

- Decompose the premium into operating cost, shareholder return, borrower risk and other permitted lending costs.
- Show how PD, LGD, EAD, expected loss, servicing cost, economic capital and competitive judgement may inform governance without being collapsed into one coefficient.
- Separate new-offer pricing, contractual repricing, limit management and customer intervention.
- Connect back to the Credit Policy and Compliance Gate.

### 3. One cash-flow engine, several measures, approximately 650 words

- Define lifecycle states and scenario cash flows.
- Introduce scheduled cash, prepayment, cure, modification, default, recovery, workout cost and closure.
- Define physical-measure forecasting and explain when market-consistent valuation is a separate optional task.
- Diagram the map from evidence to customer price, ECL, economic capital, liquidity, FTP and risk transfer.

### 4. IFRS 9 ECL follows cash shortfalls, approximately 850 words

- Separate 12-month ECL from cash losses expected during the next 12 months.
- Define lifetime ECL, probability weighting, reasonable and supportable forecasts and time value of money.
- Set out stage, SICR, default, cure, modification and write-off governance.
- Integrate discounted net recovery and qualifying contractual credit enhancement.
- Explain model-to-accounting translation, overlays, controls and journal ownership.

### 5. Economic capital prices the tail, approximately 650 words

- Separate expected loss, accounting allowance, regulatory capital and internal economic capital.
- Define a one-year portfolio loss variable and a stated VaR or expected-shortfall functional.
- Include parameter, systematic, idiosyncratic, dependence, concentration, model-form and operational uncertainty.
- Apply platform, sector, geography, supply-chain and commodity dependence.
- Describe allocation to products and relationships without claiming regulatory recognition.

### 6. Collection timing becomes liquidity, approximately 500 words

- Distinguish an accounting receivable from settled cash.
- Model contractual and behavioural maturity, collection latency, failed-payment recycling, cure and recovery lag.
- Build cash-flow ladders and stresses.
- Connect collection intervention economics to liquidity and capacity.

### 7. ALM and matched-maturity FTP, approximately 650 words

- Separate bank prudential ALM and internal FTP from product pricing and optional SPV attribution.
- Define KESONIA repricing, basis, optionality and behavioural tenor.
- Present NII and economic-value perspectives on IRRBB.
- Define a transparent matched-funding charge.
- Describe OFSAA as one optional institutional implementation, not the source of contractual truth.

### 8. Price the intervention, not only the loan, approximately 450 words

- Define causal net economic value using incremental collections, funding, treatment cost, conduct cost and capital usage.
- Avoid converting an intervention effect into a borrower risk premium.
- Set promotion requirements for treatment strategies.

### 9. Risk transfer must survive its contract, approximately 600 words

- Cover guarantees, credit insurance, first-loss arrangements, funded participation and synthetic protection.
- Define gross and net loss, attachment, exhaustion, coverage, waiting period, exclusions, claims timing, counterparty risk and recovery sharing.
- Use Kenya's Credit Guarantee Scheme as a concrete public example without generalising its terms to other programmes.
- Separate economic protection, IFRS 9 recognition and regulatory capital recognition.
- Diagram measurement, claims and monitoring.

### 10. Optional ring-fenced receivables funding, approximately 500 words

- Keep the bank balance sheet as the primary architecture.
- Present a ring-fenced SPV as an optional funding extension for eligible receivables.
- Explain legal transfer, servicing, controlled accounts, cash waterfall, reserves, OC, triggers and true-sale diligence.
- Distinguish bank FTP, asset yield, note margin and investor hurdle.

### 11. A joined financial control plane, approximately 300 words

- Close the part with an owner-and-evidence matrix.
- Hand Part 6 a measurable experimental and implementation agenda.

## Constructive language policy

The text will advance a coherent architecture. It will explain differences positively through purpose and ownership. It will not frame inherited concepts as mistakes, use adversarial disclaimers, or rely on phrases such as "not merely", "does not magically", "it would be wrong", or "this paper does not claim". Qualifications will state the conditions under which a measure, contract or method is useful.
