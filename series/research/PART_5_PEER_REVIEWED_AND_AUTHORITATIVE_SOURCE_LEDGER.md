# Part 5 Peer-Reviewed and Authoritative Source Ledger

## Purpose

This ledger records the evidence selected to deepen Part 5, *KESONIA Pricing, Expected Credit Loss, Capital and Funding*. It protects the manuscript's original architecture by separating evidence that establishes Kenyan legal or supervisory requirements from research that informs design choices. A source supports, qualifies or tests a proposition; it does not replace the paper's economic reasoning.

The source-enrichment pass follows four controls:

1. Kenyan law, CBK material and executed contracts govern Kenyan legal, regulatory and contractual claims.
2. Basel, World Bank and other official international material supplies recognised control principles, with jurisdiction stated where necessary.
3. Peer-reviewed research supplies empirical or methodological evidence. Results from another country are treated as evidence about a mechanism, not as Kenyan calibration parameters.
4. The current equations, lifecycle distinctions and institutional ownership model remain the organising spine of Part 5.

## Claim-level enrichment decisions

### Customer pricing and product response

The existing pricing bridge remains unchanged. The enrichment adds empirical support for the manuscript's proposition that a price change can alter take-up, maturity choice and portfolio composition. The source is fused into the reasoning by requiring price, tenor, acceptance and loss outcomes to be tested together. No foreign elasticity is inserted into the Kenya model.

### Lifecycle modelling and artificial intelligence

The manuscript already assigns artificial-intelligence outputs a bounded role inside a governed financial architecture. The added research supports regime-aware and segment-aware challenger testing. It does not invite a replacement of the HLR, the explicit feature path, the residual representation design or human and policy gates.

### ECL and capital

The ESRB evidence is used to explain why forward-looking allowances, capital planning and credit-cycle stress should be reconciled while retaining different measures. Gordy supports the common-factor and portfolio-granularity discussion. Tasche supports capital contributions and risk-adjusted performance attribution. The paper's one-year loss distribution, scenario framework and equations remain primary.

### Payments and Treasury recognition

Kenyan payment law establishes when settlement is final and irrevocable. The manuscript advances this legal event into a fuller Treasury recognition chain by adding availability, restriction, allocation and reconciliation. The source therefore anchors one boundary while the paper retains its original operational innovation.

### Intervention economics

The field experiments are deliberately presented together. They show that communications and repayment design can change outcomes, but also that effects depend on treatment, borrower population and outcome horizon. This directly supports Part 5's requirement to separate accelerated cash, incremental cash, durable cure, re-default and customer outcomes, with causal estimation deferred to Part 6.

### ALM and FTP

The BIS liquidity-transfer-pricing study supports the paper's matched-maturity, two-sided and centrally governed design. The deposits-channel study explains why deposit rates and balances can react to monetary policy and market structure. Its role is to motivate local estimation and stress testing, not to set Kenya assumptions. Oracle remains one possible implementation mechanism after the control model is defined.

### Credit guarantees and risk transfer

The World Bank principles and cross-country research strengthen the paper's treatment of additionality, risk-based pricing, claims, sustainability and recovery. The Kenyan regulations continue to control the local scheme. The manuscript's gross, covered and net retained distributions remain the analytical framework.

### Ring-fenced receivables funding

Kenyan law and regulations establish the legal framework. Academic securitisation evidence adds a useful economic insight: formal separation does not eliminate sponsor, servicer or implicit-support relevance. This supports the manuscript's backup-servicing, commingling, controlled-account and scenario treatment without weakening the SPV concept.

### Model governance

The current 2026 U.S. interagency guidance is used only as a comparative source for intended-use discipline. Part 5 keeps its own multi-owner approval architecture and Kenyan accountability. The citation demonstrates that intended purpose, validation proportionality, monitoring and limitations are recognised model-risk practices, while the manuscript extends them across pricing, ECL, capital, Treasury and SPV use.

## Acceptance controls

- All new sources must be cited at the claim they support.
- Reference numbering must follow first appearance in the body.
- Each retained reference must be cited and each in-text citation must resolve.
- Jurisdictional qualifications must remain visible.
- No external empirical coefficient may become a Kenyan assumption without local evidence and approval.
- No legal proposition may rely solely on peer-reviewed or commercial commentary.
- No paragraph may be rewritten merely to accommodate a citation.
- The source-enriched Part 5 must preserve the original equations, figures, institutional boundaries and transaction logic unless a verified source reveals a direct error.
