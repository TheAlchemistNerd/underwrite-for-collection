# Source and Citation Policy

## Authority order

1. Kenyan legislation, regulations, official gazettes, regulators and public institutions.
2. International standard setters and official methodological sources.
3. Original peer-reviewed research and authoritative academic monographs.
4. Official technology documentation for implementation claims.
5. Reputable professional analysis and established media for context.
6. Social posts, aggregators and AI summaries only as research leads or objects of discussion.

## IEEE sequence

Each part will use an independent IEEE numerical sequence ordered by first appearance. A series-level source index will map repeated authorities across parts, but publication numbers will follow each manuscript’s body order.

Every in-text citation must resolve to one reference entry. Every reference entry must be cited. References begin on a new page in rendered outputs, and each numbered entry begins a separate paragraph.

## Web research record

For each URL, record:

- original and canonical URL;
- title and responsible organisation or authors;
- publication and access dates;
- document type and jurisdiction;
- access result;
- claims supported;
- claims not supported;
- limitations and replacement sources;
- parts and claim IDs using the source.

Time-sensitive regulatory, benchmark and institutional claims must be rechecked during the relevant part’s drafting phase.

## Citation language

Sources will support the proposition actually stated. An aggregate association will not be cited as proof of an individual causal effect. A foreign legal or supervisory framework will be presented as a comparator, not Kenyan authority. A vendor document will support product capabilities, not independent evidence that implementation is effective.
